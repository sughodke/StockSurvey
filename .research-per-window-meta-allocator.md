# Per-window meta-allocator probe — research brief

Date: 2026-05-23
Script: `apps/docs/scripts/per_window_meta_allocator.py`

Not committed — research-only probe.

## TL;DR

The **oracle** (H1, future-peeking) on the date-aligned competitive set
(DCA + gate-v0 + vol-v3-dolthub-c200) clears the `confirmed-OOS` bar:
**ΔSR_ann = +1.43, 95% CI [+1.03, +2.05], n=261 blocks, excludes 0**.
Every **causal** rule (H2 lagged-Sharpe, H3 VIX gate, H4 Markowitz)
falls within noise: ΔSR_ann between +0.10 and +0.26 with CIs that
either include 0 or have a lower bound at the noise floor (+0.007).
**The meta-allocator dream is alive in principle (oracle clears the
bar) but dead in practice unless someone finds a much better
selection signal than "trailing Sharpe" or "VIX above median".**

The most plausible deployable angle is H3-with-better-gating: the VIX
gate's point estimate (+0.10) plus the fact that its CI bound just
barely excludes 0 hints there's *some* signal in regime-conditioning,
but the gap between H1 (+1.43) and H3 (+0.10) is the cost of using
a noisy proxy for "is vol-v3 going to win this block?". The 13× gap
says the proxy throws away ~93% of the available oracle alpha.

## Window-level overlap audit

| arc | valid blocks / 261 | date range (effective) | alignment basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| dca | 261 | 2005-02 → 2025-10 | explicit (PassiveEW daily) |
| gate-v0 | 232 | 2005-02 → 2023-08 | explicit (`dates` in NPZ) |
| vol-v3-dolthub-c200 | 29 | 2023-08 → 2025-12 | explicit (`rebal_dates` in NPZ) |
| relational-analog | 261 (TAIL-aligned) | unknown | tail-aligned to DCA — see caveat |
| factor-5d-LO-skip1 | 234 (TAIL-aligned) | unknown | tail-aligned to DCA — see caveat |

The crucial structural fact: **gate-v0 and vol-v3 are nearly
disjoint in time** (gate ends 2023-08, vol-v3 starts 2023-08). So
the per-block oracle is, for ~203 of 261 blocks, choosing between
DCA and gate-v0 (which are highly correlated — gate is DCA with a
DD overlay), and for the remaining 29 blocks choosing between DCA
and vol-v3. The "selection" is really two single-arc-vs-DCA tests
glued together by an oracle that always picks the right one.

## Tail-alignment trap (made visible)

When I included `relational-analog` and `factor-5d-LO` via
tail-alignment (their NPZs lack explicit dates), the H1 oracle
inflated to ΔSR_ann = +2.21 — but the **CI widened to [−2.50, +3.28]**,
so even the inflated point estimate doesn't pass the bar. More
importantly the lift is illusory: tail-aligning a no-dates daily
stream to DCA's last 1241 days puts the arc on the wrong calendar
blocks, so the per-block "max return" oracle picks the arc whose
distribution has the fattest right tail, not the one that actually
won that block in reality. This is exactly the inflation I was
worried about and matches the earlier ensemble-discovery failures
in `.research-leaderboard-ensembles.md`.

The H4 Markowitz number in the "all five arcs" variant was
+5.67 ΔSR_ann — clearly preposterous, same root cause: it overweights
the tail-aligned `relational` stream during what happens to be its
strongest tail-aligned window. I dropped the tail-aligned arcs from
the headline table for this reason.

## Per-hypothesis interpretation

### H1 oracle — confirmed > 0 by construction, but lift is modular

The +1.43 ΔSR_ann beats DCA at 95% with room. **But mechanistically
this is "use vol-v3 in 2023-2026 + use gate or DCA before that"**,
not a richly time-varying mixing rule. The oracle has only 1 real
"choice point" in the dataset: when vol-v3 turns on (2023-08), pick
it. Before then, gate-v0 and DCA are too correlated for the oracle
to extract much. That's why the lift is bounded near the per-arc
individual lifts (vol_v3 alone vs DCA = +1.68 over 29 blocks).
A diversified oracle across truly independent arcs would lift more,
but the date-aligned set we have doesn't contain truly independent
arcs.

### H2 lagged-Sharpe — confirmed null (k=3, k=6)

Both lookbacks produce ΔSR_ann ≈ +0.2 with CI including 0. The
selection signal (trailing 3-or-6-block Sharpe) is too noisy to
beat the "stay in DCA" naive rule. This was the prior — selection
of noisy signals adds selection cost on top of the signal noise.

### H3 VIX-regime — marginal, but borderline

ΔSR_ann = +0.10 with CI [+0.007, +1.44]. The lower bound is
technically positive but the value (+0.007) is at the noise floor;
the wide upper bound (+1.44) reflects that in the ~29 blocks where
vol_v3 is also active, the gate occasionally picks it well. **In
practice this CI excludes 0 in a way that wouldn't survive Bonferroni
correction across the 4 hypotheses we tested** — 4 tests at 5% gives
effective α ≈ 0.18 per test, and the +0.007 lower bound would
include 0 at α=0.013. I would NOT promote this to a deployment claim
on this evidence.

### H4 Markowitz — confirmed null

+0.22 ΔSR_ann with CI [−0.07, +0.56]. Standard MV-on-noisy-inputs
result: trailing 12-block covariance is too unstable to beat naive
selection. The known curse-of-dimensionality story; this is the
canonical failure mode for portfolio optimization on small samples.

## Caveats

1. **Selection cost not charged**: I did not subtract a turnover
   cost for the meta-allocator switching between arcs. Each switch
   costs ≥10 bps in practice (one full reposition between two
   different strategies). For H2 (which switches ~every 6 blocks on
   noisy signals), this would likely cost another 0.2–0.4 SR ann.
   H3 (binary, infrequent switches) would lose less. H1 oracle's
   lift is large enough to absorb this; the causal rules are not.
2. **Regime sample size**: vol-v3 has only 29 valid blocks. Any
   inference about "VIX-regime gating works" rests on 29 obs total
   and ~half of those (15-ish) being in the fired-VIX regime. The
   CI on H3 reflects this — its upper bound is +1.44, an enormous
   range, because the n=29 sample dominates uncertainty.
3. **No multiple-testing deflation across H1–H4**: I tested 4
   hypotheses on the same data, so the effective per-hypothesis α
   is ~1.3% not 5%. The H3 result fails that bar; H1 passes it
   easily (CI lower bound +1.03 ≫ noise floor).
4. **Tail-alignment of `relational` and `factor_5d_LO` is
   inadmissible.** If we want to include them, the deliverable is
   a re-dump of those arcs' streams with explicit dates, not more
   alignment tricks. Their inclusion in any meta-allocator
   conclusion would need that fix first.
5. **DCA was used as both the baseline AND a competitor in the
   oracle**. That's correct — the oracle MUST be able to "pick DCA"
   in blocks where neither overlay is active. But it means the
   oracle's lift over DCA is bounded by the union of "blocks where
   gate beat DCA" + "blocks where vol_v3 beat DCA", which is a
   small fraction of the 261 blocks.

## Honest verdict

If you wanted to write a leaderboard row for this it would be:

> `2026-05-23 | docs | per-window meta-allocator (oracle H1) |
> dca+gate+vol-v3 | block20 | ΔSR ; 95% LW CI | n=261 ;
> +1.43 ann ; [+1.03, +2.05] | confirmed-OOS (oracle, future-peeking) |
> Output/per-window-meta-allocator.json`

But — and this is the load-bearing caveat — **the oracle clearance
does not generalize to deployment**. The causal selection rules
that someone could actually run live (H2, H3, H4) are all
verdict-null or noise-floor. The oracle's lift comes from a single
calendar transition (vol-v3 turning on in 2023-08), not from a
richly time-varying alpha.

My read: **the meta-allocator hypothesis is not the next workstream
to bet on.** The prior failures in `.research-leaderboard-ensembles.md`
(0/91 additive combos beat DCA) plus this probe (3/4 causal rules
null) plus the variance-algebra argument together say: selection
across noisy partial-OOS arcs doesn't escape the noise. The right
next move is to **search for a single new arc with structural
edge**, not to mix the existing ones.

Two small follow-ups worth doing if anyone wants to extend:

- **Re-dump relational + factor with explicit dates.** Once those
  arcs have `dates` arrays in their NPZs, redo H1-H4 on the wider
  competitive set. Even if it doesn't change the verdict, it
  removes the tail-alignment caveat and makes the result publishable.
- **H3.5: build a per-block ΔSR(vol_v3 vs DCA) ~ macro-feature
  regression on the 29 vol_v3 obs.** If you can predict
  `block_ret_vol_v3 − block_ret_dca` from features available at
  block start with even a weak (R² > 0.05) fit, the H3 lift floor
  jumps. But n=29 makes this a fishing expedition — preregister
  the feature list before running.
