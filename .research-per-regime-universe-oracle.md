# Per-regime universe oracle (in-sample / future-peeking) — research probe

**Status:** read-only descriptive research. NOT a deployable rule.
**Companion to:** `.research-per-window-meta-allocator.md` (already-falsified
causal selection). This probe answers the *upper bound* question: if we knew
the regime boundaries perfectly, how interpretable is the
"regime → winning universe" mapping?

**Date:** 2026-05-23
**Script:** `apps/docs/scripts/per_regime_universe_oracle.py`
**JSON output:** `Output/per-regime-universe-oracle.json`
**Chart:** `apps/docs/docs/findings/images/per-regime-universe-oracle.png`

---

## TL;DR

**The mapping is mostly noise.** With 45 ≥21-TD regimes at L=252 evaluated
against 19 curated universes, **no single regime class concentrates winners
into one family with conviction.** The strongest pattern is "low-VIX favors
equity-tilt (QQQ/IWM)" at 6/13 = 46% — but the *modal* VIX class is mid-VIX
(27/45 regimes) and its winner-family entropy is 2.63 bits out of a 2.81-bit
ceiling (94% of max). The recession crosstab has only 5 in-recession regimes,
too few to support a rule. **The descriptive answer matches the causal
finding from the meta-allocator probe: regime-conditioning the universe is
not the lever.**

---

## What was evaluated

- **Regime list:** 45 persistent (≥21-TD) regimes from
  `Output/regimes-since-2005.json` at L=252 rolling Sharpe (the canonical
  list from the regimes-since-2005 brief).
- **Universes (19 curated):** SPY, VTI, QQQ, IWM, 9-SPDR-sectors-EW;
  TLT, IEF, AGG; GLD, DBC; canonical-13ETF, winner-4ETF, 60/40 (SPY+AGG),
  golden-butterfly, all-weather; risk-on (SPY+QQQ+EFA), growth
  (XLK+XLY+XLC); risk-off (TLT+IEF+GLD); defensives (XLP+XLU+XLV).
- **Engine:** equal-weight, `rebal_days=20`, `commission_bps=10`, L1-turnover
  commission. Matches the `cfr.baselines.PassiveEW` convention from
  `dca_returns_dump.py`.
- **Price data:** `cfr_phase4d_multiasset_close.pkl` (SPDR sectors + TLT/IEF
  /GLD/DBC, continuous from 2005-02-25) augmented with SPY/VTI/QQQ/IWM/EFA
  /AGG/XLC from `StooqData/`. XLC's first-valid is 2018-06-21 → the growth
  universe (XLK+XLY+XLC) is unavailable for pre-2018 regimes and dropped
  there. All other universes are continuously available 2005-on.
- **Selection rule:** for each regime, restrict the price panel to the
  regime's `[start, end]` range, simulate each candidate universe over those
  bars, take the universe with the highest annualized Sharpe. Oracle.

A handful of observations are *interpretable*: the 2007-11→2008-09 GFC
window picked **DBC-only** (oil/commodities ran hard before the crash);
the 2018-04→2018-12 risk-off window picked **defensives**; the 2024-04→
2025-12 window (mostly the current commodity/gold rally) picked
**GLD-only**. The 2022-06→2023-02 bear window picked **9-SPDR-sectors-EW**
— most universes were negative; equity-sectors-EW had the smallest negative.

But there are also **degenerate / over-fit picks**: 25-30 TD regimes
routinely picked single-asset universes (TLT, GLD, IEF) with absurd
in-sample annualized Sharpes (+3 to +7). These are pure window-fitting
artifacts — a single asset that happened to run for 25 days in a row will
look unbeatable on a 25-day window.

The only *kind-of* interpretable pattern is **low-VIX → equity-tilt** at
6/13 = 46%, beating uniform (1/7 = 14%) by 3.2×. But equity-tilt = {QQQ,
IWM} which mechanically is "the most volatile equity bucket in the menu";
that wins in calm markets because there's no downside to volatility-as-
return. Not surprising, not actionable beyond "if VIX is low, lean QQQ" —
which is already conventional wisdom.

High/extreme VIX (combined n=5) is too small to read. The fact that
extreme-VIX picks include one bonds and one commodities (and no
defensives, no risk-off) is sample-size noise.

## Recession / Fed-tightening crosstab

| context | IN regimes | OUT regimes |
|---|---|---|
| NBER recession overlap | 5 | 40 |
| Fed-tightening overlap | 12 | 33 |

**Recession (in n=5):** commodities=2, multi-asset=2, bonds=1.
Conventional wisdom would predict bonds/defensives; the in-sample data
disagrees because the two NBER episodes in the window (2007-12→2009-06
and 2020-02→2020-04) had wildly different inside dynamics — early-GFC
was commodities running, late-GFC was bonds, 2020-Q1 was a brief crash
that didn't last long enough to land any meaningful regime winners.

**Fed-tightening (in n=12):** roughly uniform across families
(equity=3, bonds=2, equity-tilt=2, defensive=2, commodities=2, multi=1).
No discernible pattern.

## Entropy of winner distribution per regime class

(Max entropy at 7 observed families = 2.81 bits.)

| VIX class | n | H (bits) | H / Hmax | dominant family |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| low     | 13 | 2.046 | 0.73 | equity-tilt (46%) |
| mid     | 27 | **2.628** | **0.94** | commodities (26%) |
| high    | 3  | 0.918 | 0.33 | multi-asset (67%) |
| extreme | 2  | 1.000 | 0.36 | tied |

The modal regime class (mid-VIX, 60% of sample) sits at 94% of maximum
entropy — i.e., **the winning universe in a typical regime is essentially
uniformly random across families.** The "tighter" classes (high, extreme)
have low entropy only because their sample sizes are 3 and 2.

## Stability — does the same universe win in adjacent regimes of the same class?

Looking at the per-regime table: across 10 mid-VIX dca_winner_4etf-original
regimes, the in-sample winners are **{TLT-only, IEF-only, golden-butterfly,
winner-4ETF, AGG-only, defensives, canonical-13ETF, QQQ-only, IWM-only,
GLD-only}** — every regime picks a different universe. Even conditioning
on identical original-strategy-winner + identical VIX class, the picked
universe is not stable. This is the strongest single piece of evidence
that the in-sample winners are **window-specific noise rather than a
discoverable regime → universe mapping**.

## 3-5 most common "regime class → winning family" rules (with frequency)

In descending order of frequency / interpretability:

1. **low-VIX → equity-tilt (QQQ/IWM)** — 6/13 low-VIX regimes (46%).
   The strongest pattern in the data, but the obvious-from-textbook one.
2. **mid-VIX → commodities** — 7/27 (26%). Driven heavily by the
   2006-2008 and 2021-2022 commodity bull runs; not a regime rule, a
   sample-period rule.
3. **NBER recession → commodities or multi-asset** — 4/5 (80%) of
   in-recession regimes. Driven by 2008 commodity blow-off and brief
   2020 multi-asset rebalancing windows. Counter-intuitive and likely
   sample-specific.
4. **mid-VIX → bonds** — 6/27 (22%). Mostly TLT/IEF "Fed-pivot" windows
   (2014-11, 2015-04, 2015-10, 2018-12).
5. **(No defensives / risk-off concentration anywhere)** — the
   risk-off basket wins exactly *once* across all 45 regimes (low Sharpe
   universes don't win in-sample even in stressed windows, because they
   lose less but other arms lose less still).

Rules 2-4 are *post-hoc descriptions*, not regime-conditional alpha.

## Caveats (the important part)

1. **In-sample by construction.** Every Sharpe reported is computed on the
   same window used to pick the winner. This *cannot* be deployed.
2. **Short-regime artifact.** 25-30 TD regimes routinely picked
   single-asset universes with annualized Sharpes >+4 because a single
   asset that runs uninterrupted for 25 days will dominate any basket
   over that window. This is fitting the window, not the regime.
3. **Causal selection has already been shown to fail.** The
   meta-allocator probe (`.research-per-window-meta-allocator.md`) tried
   causal regime-detection rules (H2 trailing-Sharpe, H3 VIX gate, H4
   LBO) and none cleared the 95% CI vs the canonical DCA arc. This probe
   establishes the *ceiling* (oracle pick) on the same problem and
   confirms that even the ceiling lacks interpretable structure inside
   regime classes.
4. **The L=252 rolling-Sharpe regimes are themselves derived from arc
   returns, not from macro conditions.** A "regime" in this brief is
   "the window in which one of {dca, gate, pairs, relational,
   dca_winner_4etf, vol_v3} had the highest 252-day trailing Sharpe."
   That's a *measurement-class* regime, not a macro-class regime. The
   VIX/NBER overlays are post-hoc descriptions of these
   measurement-class regimes, not their definition.
5. **Per-window oracle ΔSR vs canonical-13ETF baseline NOT computed.**
   This brief intentionally stops at "winner family" and does not
   compute the oracle's per-window alpha over a fixed baseline. The
   meta-allocator brief already did the equivalent (H1 oracle arc-pick
   +1.43 ann ΔSR), and we expect a similar order of magnitude here. The
   point of this brief is the *interpretability* of the picks, not their
   collective alpha.

## What this changes

Nothing operationally. The canonical-13ETF DCA basket
(`apps/dca`) remains the deployed strategy. The meta-allocator finding
(causal regime-selection across {DCA, vol-v3, relational, ...} fails the
95% CI) is reinforced by this companion finding (causal regime-selection
across universes would also fail, because even the oracle picks are not
stable within regime classes).

## Suggested next experiments

Per the CLAUDE.md "propose what's next" protocol for a `confirmed-null`
verdict (orthogonal lever, not another variation):

1. **Different prediction problem.** Stop trying to predict "which arm
   wins" and instead predict "which arm *fails badly*" (a binary stop-loss
   gate). The asymmetry — disaster-detection is easier than alpha-ranking
   — is the orthogonal axis. Same arc panel, different prediction target.
2. **Universe-level vol-targeting** rather than universe-selection. The
   canonical 13-ETF passes through realized vol unmodulated; an
   ex-ante-vol-targeted version of the same basket may be more
   informative than which basket to pick.
3. **Defer this question entirely** until a new arc lands that introduces
   a structurally-different return profile from the existing six. The
   regime-selection problem is bounded by the menu diversity; the current
   menu is six members that are all variations on "long stocks ± mild
   risk gate" — there's no orthogonal arm to *route to*.
