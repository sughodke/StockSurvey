# Regime forecasting for strategy allocation — literature brief

Scope: what the published literature says about (a) defining regimes,
(b) forecasting them, (c) using forecasts to allocate across N
strategies, with explicit attention to negative results and to our
specific n=45-regime, 6-arc situation.

## 1. Canonical framings of "regime" for allocation

The literature carries three operationally distinct uses of the word
"regime", and conflating them is the most common methodological error.

- **Latent state-switching (Hamilton 1989, "A New Approach to the
  Economic Analysis of Nonstationary Time Series and the Business
  Cycle", *Econometrica*).** Regime is an unobserved discrete state
  governing the parameters of an AR process; inferred by filtering.
  Hamilton's original 2-state AR(4) on US GNP recovers the NBER
  recession dating in-sample. Empirically it does *not* survive
  sample extension past 1984 (the variance-shift in the Great
  Moderation breaks the identification). Framing is **observational/
  filtered**, not predictive.
- **Multivariate regime-switching for asset returns (Ang & Bekaert
  2002, "International Asset Allocation With Regime Shifts",
  *RFS* 15:1137; Guidolin & Timmermann 2007, "Asset Allocation Under
  Multivariate Regime Switching", *JEDC*).** Two-regime
  normal/bear (Ang-Bekaert) and four-regime crash/slow/bull/recovery
  (Guidolin-Timmermann) calibrated on stock+bond joint distribution.
  Both report OOS allocation gains versus static
  mean-variance — but the gains concentrate in the bear/crash state
  switch, and Guidolin-Timmermann's K=4 is at the edge of what
  monthly post-war data identifies. Framing is **predictive**
  (filtered state probability used at t to allocate t+1).
- **Macro-state stacks (Bridgewater "All Weather"; Faber GTAA
  2007/2013).** Regime is a *deterministic function* of observable
  features (growth × inflation cells; or 10-month MA above/below).
  No latent state, no inference — the "regime" is engineered.
  This is what `ss_macro` enables and is closest to our 6-feature
  stack. Framing is **detection / classification**, not forecasting.

The fourth framing — **CSCV / DSR-style protocols** (Bailey, Borwein,
López de Prado, Zhu 2014; Bailey & López de Prado 2014 *The
Deflated Sharpe Ratio*) — is not regime-detection; it is the protocol
under which any regime claim should be evaluated. The relevant
operating rule is that selection bias inflates Sharpe roughly as
sqrt(2 log N) for N effectively-independent trials. With our n=45
regimes the trial count is small but selection-of-best-arc-per-regime
is exactly the operation DSR was designed to deflate.

## 2. Forecasting techniques with published OOS support

- **HMM / Gaussian-mixture regime-switching.** Ang-Bekaert (2002)
  and Guidolin-Timmermann (2007) carry the strongest published OOS
  results — both report utility gains net of transaction costs at K=2
  and K=4 respectively over ~30 years of monthly data. Replications
  by Kritzman, Page, Turkington (FAJ 2012) find the gain concentrates
  in regime-2 (turbulence) and is fragile to K. Recent Gaussian-HMM
  literature (Cube Exchange survey; van de Bunt et al. on repulsive
  priors, arXiv 2024) consistently warns that K>3 on monthly equity
  data overfits.
- **BOCPD (Adams & MacKay 2007, "Bayesian Online Changepoint
  Detection", arXiv 0710.3742).** Demonstrated on DJIA 1972–1975 in
  the original; extended to portfolio applications by recent ACM
  ICMLT 2025 work and arXiv 2407.16376 (Bayesian autoregressive
  BOCPD with time-varying parameters). The published claim is
  detection, not forecast: regime *change* is identifiable in
  near-real-time but the *new* regime's character is unknown until
  more data accumulates. This is the "regime detection is always
  lagged" lower bound, and it is structural — you cannot estimate a
  new regime's mean before drawing from it.
- **Macro conditioning — yield curve / VIX cycles.** Hansen 2024
  ("Predicting recessions using VIX–yield curve cycles", *Int J
  Forecasting* 40:409) shows the joint VIX/slope counterclockwise
  cycle outperforms the slope alone for 6/12/18-month recession
  probit OOS 1950–2022. Equity-return predictability from the same
  stack is weaker (Welch-Goyal 2008; Goyal-Welch-Zafirov 2024
  "A Comprehensive 2022 Look", *RFS* 37:3490 — of 46 published
  predictors, ≈1/3 are no longer in-sample significant and half of
  the survivors have negative OOS R² over the post-2008 update).
- **Combination forecasts.** Rapach, Strauss, Zhou 2010,
  "Out-of-Sample Equity Premium Prediction: Combination Forecasts
  and Links to the Real Economy", *RFS* 23:821 — simple-mean
  combination of 15 individual predictive regressions delivers
  statistically and economically significant OOS R² where individual
  predictors fail. Replicated (with attenuation) in subsequent work.
  This is the most empirically robust positive result in the
  regime-conditioning literature.
- **Trend-following / TS-momentum as regime proxy.** Faber 2007
  GTAA (10-month MA on 5 asset classes): published OOS 2006–2012
  delivers Sharpe ≈0.6 with ~80% DD reduction vs buy-and-hold.
  Moskowitz, Ooi, Pedersen 2012 ("Time Series Momentum", *JFE*)
  generalizes to 58 instruments. The decade since publication has
  been famously hostile (2011–2020), see Hurst-Ooi-Pedersen "A
  Century of Evidence" for the steel-man.
- **Recent ML.** Transformer/LSTM regime classifiers exist
  (arXiv 2603.19136 *Adaptive Regime-Aware Stock Price Prediction*;
  arXiv 2410.22346 *Representation Learning for Regime Detection*)
  but none have a published walk-forward Sharpe meeting CSCV
  standards. Treat as research-grade, not deployable.

## 3. Meta-allocation across N strategies

- **Style timing / factor rotation.** Asness, Chandra, Ilmanen,
  Israel 2017 "Contrarian Factor Timing Is Deceptively Difficult"
  and Asness 2016 "The Siren Song of Factor Timing" are the canonical
  negative results: in-sample value-spread-conditioned style timing
  shows large gains, but OOS the gains are eaten by transaction
  costs and the diversification of static factor exposure beats the
  timed portfolio. Brown-Goetzmann 1995 ("Mutual Fund Styles", *JFE*)
  is the older positive in-sample analog.
- **Meta-labeling (López de Prado 2018, *Advances in Financial
  Machine Learning*, Ch. 3).** Triple-barrier + secondary
  classifier on whether to *act* on a primary signal. Hudson-Thames
  replication (Singh & Joubert 2022) finds meaningful precision/F1
  gains. This is the most direct analog to our 6-arc allocation
  problem: train one model per arc to decide
  "should-this-arc-trade-now", then size by predicted confidence.
- **Risk parity / HRP as regime-naive alternatives.** López de
  Prado 2016 HRP (*JPM*) and the Bridgewater All-Weather framing
  both argue that the right response to regime uncertainty is *not*
  to forecast it but to be balanced across it. HRP OOS Sharpe beats
  inverse-variance and mean-variance in López de Prado's
  simulations; All-Weather has live track record but no
  peer-reviewed OOS.

## 4. What does NOT work

- **In-sample regime fitting → OOS collapse.** Bailey, Borwein,
  López de Prado, Zhu 2014, "The Probability of Backtest
  Overfitting": with M trials, the SR of the best in-sample
  candidate is inflated by ≈ E[max] of M standard normals. Regime
  hyperparameters (K, window length, feature set) compound this.
- **HMM K>3 on monthly equity data.** ~240–300 monthly obs cannot
  identify >3 means + >3 variances + a 3×3 transition matrix
  reliably; multiple replications (Kritzman et al.; Cube survey)
  converge on K=2 or K=3.
- **Macro→equity-return predictors at horizons <1y.** Goyal-Welch
  2008 and Goyal-Welch-Zafirov 2024 are the standing reference.
- **Style/factor timing.** Asness papers above. The OOS record is
  flat-to-negative net of costs.
- **Regime detection is intrinsically lagged.** Information-theoretic
  lower bound (and BOCPD's run-length posterior makes it explicit):
  you cannot estimate a new regime's mean below the noise floor of
  the new regime's draws until enough draws accumulate. For monthly
  data with σ=4%, ~10 obs are needed to detect a 1σ mean shift at
  α=0.05 — i.e., ~10 months of lag.

## 5. Sample-size constraints for n=45 regimes

A 6×6 transition matrix has 30 free parameters (6 rows × 5 free
entries). 44 transitions across 45 regimes ≈ 1.5 observations per
free parameter — well below the "10 obs per parameter" textbook
floor for stable MLE (no specific cite found; this is folklore
extrapolated from logistic-regression sample-size rules of thumb,
Peduzzi et al. 1996 in epidemiology). Laplace smoothing (α=1) is
mandatory; the unsmoothed transition matrix will have multiple
zero rows. For a regime-switching VAR with 6 macro features the
effective parameter count balloons further — Guidolin-Timmermann's
K=4 used 30 years of monthly data (360 obs) and they regard it as
the empirical ceiling.

## 6. What a methodology referee would say

Given (n=45, 44 transitions, 6 arcs, macro stack built, in-sample
oracle universe-selection at 94% of max entropy in the modal class,
no benchmark against persistence):

1. **Benchmark first, model second.** The trailing-rolling-Sharpe-
   winner persistence baseline must be the bar. Every more-complex
   candidate (Markov, HMM, GBM-on-macro) is reported as Δ-Sharpe
   versus persistence on locked walk-forward windows. This is the
   Rapach-Strauss-Zhou and Welch-Goyal protocol.
2. **Deflate the Sharpe.** With 6 arcs and a regime-conditioning
   layer, the effective N of trials is ≥6 even before considering
   hyperparameters; apply DSR (Bailey-López de Prado 2014) before
   declaring any winner.
3. **K small or no K.** Don't fit K>3 latent regimes. Either commit
   to deterministic macro buckets (Bridgewater framing) or to K=2
   normal/turbulent (Kritzman-Page-Turkington framing).
4. **Combination beats selection.** Rapach-Strauss-Zhou's result
   that simple-mean ensemble outperforms best-single applies
   directly: a 1/6 equal-weight across arcs is a serious baseline,
   and an inverse-vol weight across arcs is a stronger one. The
   referee will ask for both alongside any regime-conditioned
   allocator.
5. **Meta-labeling, not regime classification.** Train one
   classifier per arc on "will this arc earn its full-sample Sharpe
   in the next H days?" The 6 classifiers are independently
   trainable, the label space is binary (high-DOF efficient), and
   the resulting weights are a soft-vote — closer to López de
   Prado's meta-labeling than to a hard regime-switch.
6. **Macro→universe-selection null is informative.** The in-sample
   oracle running at 94% of max entropy is strong evidence that
   *within* the VIX-modal class there is no allocator signal at
   that level of granularity. The forecasting problem must be
   re-cast at a different horizon or a different label.

## 7. Recommendation — highest-EV 2–3 techniques

(a) **Persistence baseline + 1/N + inverse-vol-of-arc as a triple
benchmark**, deflated-Sharpe-compared on a locked walk-forward.
This is methodology, not modelling — but without it nothing else
is interpretable.

(b) **Meta-labeling per arc** (López de Prado 2018 framing): one
binary classifier per arc on whether the arc is in its
high-Sharpe regime over the next H trading days, features =
`ss_macro` 6-stack + arc's own rolling Sharpe. Combine soft
predictions by inverse-vol weighting. This sidesteps the
6-class classification curse and matches the
Rapach-Strauss-Zhou combination-forecast positive result.

(c) **A 2-state Kritzman-Page-Turkington turbulence regime
overlay**, used only as a risk-off gate (cut all arc weights by
50% in turbulent state), *not* as an arc-selector. K=2 is the
only HMM specification supported by our 240-month sample, and
restricting it to a sizing overlay matches the strongest
documented OOS gain (Ang-Bekaert's "switch to cash in the bear
state").

Avoid: K=6 HMM, full 6×6 transition-matrix MLE without smoothing,
any deep-learning regime classifier without a CSCV envelope, and
any in-sample-fit best-arc-per-VIX-bucket rule (the macro→universe
null is the canonical confirmed-null on that lever).
