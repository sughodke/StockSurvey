# Walk-forward, apples-to-apples: a literature survey for the cross-arc DSR ladder

**Scope.** What does academic finance, the practitioner community, and the modern ML-for-quant literature actually prescribe as the honest apples-to-apples walk-forward methodology for comparing heterogeneous strategies across regimes? Read against `apps/docs/docs/findings/deflated-sharpe-leaderboard.md` — this brief builds on, not over, that page.

---

## 1. TL;DR — the field's consensus, where one exists

There is **no single consensus framework**. There is, however, a converged consensus on **what is wrong** with naive practice, and three competing frameworks each address a different slice of the problem:

1. **Within-strategy selection bias** (the "I tried 200 configs, what survived?" question) is owned by the **Bailey–López de Prado** ladder: PSR → DSR → MinTRL → PBO → CPCV. The DSR is the single number; CPCV is the recommended *path generator* underneath it.
2. **Multiple-strategy / cross-factor selection bias** (the "I tried 316 factors, which are real?" question) is owned by the **Harvey–Liu / Hou–Xue–Zhang** family: t-hurdle ≥ 3.0 (Harvey–Liu BH-FDR / double-bootstrap), NYSE-breakpoint value-weighting, FDR control rather than DSR. Hou–Xue–Zhang 2020 (RFS) makes 65% of 452 anomalies fail even the |t|>1.96 single-test bar; 82% fail at |t|>2.78.
3. **Pairwise / multiple-strategy Sharpe differences** (the "is A better than B?" question) is owned by the **Ledoit–Wolf 2008** studentized stationary-bootstrap CI on the Sharpe-difference, extending Jobson–Korkie–Memmel to non-normal serially-correlated returns. This is the actual canonical test the leaderboard's ranking question should be running.

The most honest *operational* recipe in the literature for ranking heterogeneous strategies — different horizons, different universes, different sample lengths, different search budgets — is to (a) align them on a **common date window** (the intersection), (b) compute pairwise Sharpe-difference Ledoit–Wolf CIs on that common window, (c) report the DSR independently as a within-arc selection-bias correction, (d) report PBO from CPCV as the overfit probability, and (e) refuse to compare DSR magnitudes across arcs with materially different `(sample_length, n_trials)` tuples — instead report the **MinTRL** to reach a target benchmark Sharpe as the cross-arc-comparable quantity.

Jensen–Kelly–Pedersen (2023) is the dissenting recent voice: a hierarchical Bayes shrinkage model treats the multiple-testing penalty differently — adjacent factors *strengthen* rather than weaken each other's evidence when they cluster into themes. Worth knowing exists, but a single-researcher workspace can't build a 153-factor hierarchical prior.

## 2. The cross-strategy comparison playbook

For the StockSurvey ladder's specific problem — DCA (5232 daily obs, 4 trials) vs vol-v3 (30 rebals, 12 trials) vs relational (≈300 rebals, 16 trials) vs factor (≈1500 days, 50 trials) — the literature prescribes the following sequence:

### Step 1 — Define the common comparison window
Compute the **date intersection** of every arc's OOS stream. The DSR ladder's current per-arc-native sample is internally honest for the "did this arc beat its own null?" question but not for "is arc A better than arc B?". Restrict every arc to the intersection window, re-annualize, and carry **two** Sharpe columns: native-window (selection-bias decision) and common-window (cross-arc decision). This is the standard convention in AQR "Value and Momentum Everywhere" (Asness–Moskowitz–Pedersen 2013) — they align eight asset classes on the shortest common history.

### Step 2 — Align return frequency
If A is daily and B is monthly, **collapse A to monthly** (sum log-returns within calendar month). Never expand B to daily — that introduces phantom autocorrelation that breaks Newey–West and Ledoit–Wolf. Daily-to-monthly aggregation is lossless for Sharpe estimation under realistic memory ≤ 1 month.

### Step 3 — Pairwise Sharpe differences via Ledoit–Wolf 2008
For every pair (A, B), build the time-aligned excess-stream `r_A - r_B` and compute the studentized stationary-bootstrap (Politis–Romano 1994, average block length ≈ 5) 95% CI for the Sharpe-difference. Report `ΔSR ± CI`. The ladder becomes a **partial order** — A > B only when zero is outside the CI. Strategies inside each others' CIs are tied, not ranked. This is the methodologically-correct cross-arc ranking statement.

### Step 4 — Selection bias: DSR as the *within-arc* gate, not the cross-arc key
Keep DSR as the **gate** ("did this arc clear its own multiple-testing-adjusted null?"). Once an arc clears `DSR > 0.95`, it enters the cross-arc Ledoit–Wolf ranking. Below the gate, it doesn't enter — selection bias means its Sharpe is an upward-biased estimate. The DSR scalar is *not* a cross-arc rank key when `n_trials` and `n_obs` differ by orders of magnitude — Bailey–López de Prado never claimed it was. The leaderboard's current usage as a ranking key is a misuse; using it as a gate is correct.

### Step 5 — MinTRL as the cross-arc-comparable scalar
Report **Bailey–López de Prado's Minimum Track Record Length** — "how many years of monthly returns would I need to distinguish this arc's true Sharpe from zero (or from a benchmark of 0.5) at 95%?" — alongside DSR. MinTRL is `(1 + (SR_obs² - skew·SR_obs + ((kurt-1)/4)·SR_obs²)) · (Z_α / (SR_obs - SR_*))²`. It collapses sample length, higher moments, and a chosen benchmark into a single comparable number with units of *years*, which is intuitive and cross-arc-comparable in a way the DSR scalar is not.

### Step 6 — Disclose `n_trials` honestly
Harvey–Liu 2020 ("False (and Missed) Discoveries"): use **double bootstrap + BH-FDR**, not Bonferroni or expected-max-SR, when arcs are correlated. The current DSR `n_trials` count is each arc's *internal* search; the **cross-arc** multiple-testing count is the *family* total (≈100+ across all arcs). At family level, the t-hurdle is 3.0+, not 2.0 — Harvey–Liu–Zhu 2016, Hou–Xue–Zhang 2020.

### Recipe checks (the user's specific questions)

| Question | Field answer |
|---|---|
| Daily vs monthly: collapse or expand? | **Collapse daily → monthly**. Expanding monthly to daily injects autocorrelation. |
| n_trials=4 vs n_trials=200: deflate by max or own? | **Each by its own** for the within-arc gate; for the cross-arc rank, neither — use Ledoit–Wolf on the difference. The DSR is *not* a cross-arc magnitude. |
| 20yr vs 3yr sample: restrict, extrapolate, or disclaim? | **Restrict A to B's window** for the rank claim. Keep the 20yr number as "native confidence" but never compare its DSR magnitude against B's DSR magnitude. |
| Is there a sample-length-independent metric? | **MinTRL** (in years) and the **PSR vs a fixed benchmark** (e.g. PSR vs SR* = 0.5) are the closest the field has. Both are scale-comparable across arcs. |
| Benchmark SR for PSR — zero, rf, S&P, peer ensemble? | López de Prado: use a **decision-relevant benchmark**. For deployable strategies, SR* = 0.5 is the common practitioner threshold ("does this beat passive equity"); SR* = 0 is the null-skill bar. Use SR* = 0.5 for the deployability decision; SR* = 0 for the existence decision. |

## 3. Cross-regime evaluation methodology

The literature converges on three approaches for strategies whose Sharpe is regime-conditional (the vol-v3, momentum-crash, gate cases):

### 3a. Pre-registered regime split (the honest answer)
Daniel–Moskowitz 2016 "Momentum Crashes" — split sample by **panic state** (market in drawdown × high vol), pre-register the regime indicator before evaluation, report the *conditional* Sharpe in each regime separately. This is what `apps/vol/v3` already does with the 126d-VIX-median gate. Generalize: **every regime-conditional arc should report (a) full-sample Sharpe, (b) fired-conditional Sharpe, (c) regime-off Sharpe, (d) regime-trigger frequency**. Three separate t-stats, not one.

### 3b. Pagan–Sossounov bull/bear dating
Pagan–Sossounov 2003 (JAE): formal rules for bull/bear regime identification (min 4-month phase, 16-month full cycle). Used by AQR, Research Affiliates for sample regime construction. Cleaner than ad-hoc vol thresholds because the regime definition predates the strategy. **The honest test of a regime-conditional arc is whether its Sharpe holds in each Pagan–Sossounov regime separately, not whether the regime gate optimizes pooled Sharpe.**

### 3c. The Cederburg et al. critique — and the response
Cederburg–O'Doherty–Wang–Yan 2020: vol-managed portfolios fail OOS because the in-sample vol-managed Sharpe lift is a sample-specific artifact. DeMiguel–Martin-Utrera–Uppal 2024: a *multifactor* conditional strategy survives. Lesson: **single-strategy regime overlays don't survive; multifactor regime overlays might**. The StockSurvey "regime-scaled DCA" experiment (2026-05-22, leaderboard) confirms — single-overlay alphas of +0.02 are inside the noise band. The field expects this failure mode.

### Operational rule for the ladder
Mark any arc with a regime gate as `regime-conditional` in the verdict column. Report DSR separately on the (gate-fired, gate-off) sub-streams. A regime-conditional arc whose conditional-fired Sharpe is unimpressive (vol-v3: ann SR +1.15 fired but DSR t +1.32 — inside the noise) is *not* salvaged by the gate.

## 4. Reference frameworks table

| Framework | What it claims | What it requires | What it misses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DSR (Bailey–LdP 2014)** | "Given the Sharpe and `n_trials`, the probability the true SR > 0." | Return stream, `n_trials`, cross-trial SR std. | Cross-arc comparability; assumes IID per-period; ignores regime shifts. |
| **PSR (Bailey–LdP 2012)** | "Probability observed SR > benchmark SR." | Return stream, benchmark SR. | Selection bias (DSR fixes this). |
| **MinTRL (Bailey–LdP 2012)** | "Years of track record needed to reject SR* at 95%." | Return stream, benchmark. | Doesn't price `n_trials`; pair with DSR. |
| **CPCV (López de Prado 2018)** | Multiple independent backtest paths via combinatorial train/test splits with purging + embargo. | Labels with horizon, embargo length. | Computationally expensive; assumes label horizon is well-defined (hard for portfolio strategies). |
| **PBO (Bailey–Borwein–LdP 2014)** | "Probability the IS-best strategy underperforms median OOS." | Matrix of IS/OOS scores across trials. | Doesn't tell you the true Sharpe — just whether your selection process is broken. |
| **CSCV (Bailey–Borwein–LdP)** | Combinatorially-symmetric CV to estimate PBO. | Trial matrix. | Same. |
| **Ledoit–Wolf 2008** | Studentized stationary-bootstrap CI for ΔSR between two streams. | Two aligned return streams. | Pairwise; doesn't handle many-strategy FWER (use Romano–Wolf for that). |
| **Romano–Wolf step-down** | FWER control across many strategies, respecting dependence. | Trial matrix + bootstrap. | More conservative than FDR; computationally expensive. |
| **Harvey–Liu 2020 double bootstrap** | FDR control across many strategies. | Trial matrix. | Requires assumption that nulls and alternatives are bootstrap-stable. |
| **SPA (Hansen 2005) / Reality Check (White 2000)** | "Is the best of N strategies better than benchmark, adjusted for searching N?" | N candidate streams + benchmark. | Tests existence, not magnitude. |
| **Jobson–Korkie–Memmel** | Pairwise ΔSR test under normality + IID. | Two streams. | Fails on fat tails / autocorrelation. Use Ledoit–Wolf instead. |
| **Hou–Xue–Zhang 2020 / Harvey–Liu–Zhu 2016** | "Cross-section anomaly t-hurdle is 2.78–3.0, not 2.0." | Universe of factor tests. | Cross-sectional only; not directly a strategy ladder. |
| **Jensen–Kelly–Pedersen 2023** | Hierarchical Bayes shrinkage across factor clusters. | Many correlated factor streams. | Requires ≥100 strategies clustered into themes; not a single-arc tool. |
| **CSPA (Cederburg et al. 2020)** | Conditional Sharpe Performance Adjustment for regime-conditional arcs. | Pre-registered regime indicator. | Requires a regime split known ex-ante. |

## 5. The honest critique of DSR alone

The DSR ladder as currently used in `deflated-sharpe-leaderboard.md` has six weaknesses the field would flag:

1. **DSR is a within-arc selection-bias correction, not a cross-arc ranking key.** Bailey–López de Prado never claim it is. The current ladder uses the deflated-t magnitude to rank arcs whose `(n_obs, n_trials)` differ by an order of magnitude — that comparison is not well-defined. Vol-v3 at `(30, 12)` and DCA at `(5232, 4)` have deflated-t numbers that aren't on the same scale even after the calibration fix.
2. **DSR assumes IID per-period returns.** No regime conditioning. For arcs with structural breaks (vol-v3's pre-/post-GFC regime, factor's mega-cap-specificity) the IID assumption is wrong. Pagan–Sossounov-style regime splits are the recommended fix.
3. **DSR doesn't handle the family-level multiple-testing problem.** The leaderboard has ~100 rows. Even if each arc clears its own DSR gate, the family probability of a false positive is much higher. The Harvey–Liu BH-FDR or Romano–Wolf step-down would handle this; DSR alone does not.
4. **`n_trials` is reconstructed, not observed.** The leaderboard's `n_trials` values (4, 6, 12, 16, 50) are post-hoc reconstructions. Best practice (López de Prado): log every configuration tested, including failed smoke tests and abandoned arms, in a `trial_log.jsonl` per arc. The current approach almost certainly under-counts.
5. **DSR assumes the cross-trial Sharpe std is known.** The 0.25 workspace-empirical calibration is reasonable but it's a single point estimate. The honest version is to bootstrap the cross-trial std and propagate the uncertainty into the DSR confidence interval — Ledoit–Wolf style.
6. **DSR has no notion of regime-conditional sub-streams.** A regime-conditional arc whose fired-conditional Sharpe is impressive but whose unconditional Sharpe is mediocre gets the same DSR as a non-conditional arc. Daniel–Moskowitz would split.

López de Prado himself is consistent: "Backtesting is not a research tool" (chapter 7, *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*). The DSR is a backtest-overfitting diagnostic, not a strategy-selection oracle. It should always travel with CPCV (path-generation), PBO (overfit probability), and a multi-strategy correction (Romano–Wolf or Harvey–Liu).

## 6. Recommendations for the StockSurvey ladder

Concrete, deployable, no-paid-data, no-pytorch changes. Ordered by ROI:

### R1 (highest ROI) — Add a common-window Sharpe-difference column to the ladder
Compute the **date intersection** across all six stream arcs (likely 2017–2025 — DCA + relational + vol + factor + pairs + gate all overlap). Restrict every arc to this window. Add a `common_window_sharpe` column and a `vs_DCA_ΔSR (95% CI)` column using Ledoit–Wolf 2008 studentized stationary bootstrap. **This is the apples-to-apples ranking the user is asking for.** Implementation: `apps/docs/scripts/compute_common_window.py`. Effort: 1 day.

### R2 — Add a MinTRL column
For each arc, compute Bailey–López de Prado MinTRL at SR* = 0 (existence) and SR* = 0.5 (deployability). Report both. MinTRL is the closest thing to a cross-arc-comparable scalar in the literature — it carries units of years, which is intuitive. Add to `ss_portfolio.standardize_oos`'s `MetricBlock`. Effort: 0.5 day (the math is already in `standardize_oos`).

### R3 — Promote DSR from rank key to gate
Stop ranking by deflated-t. Use `DSR > 0.95` as a **gate**: arcs below the gate are tagged "selection-bias-suspect", arcs above are entered into the Ledoit–Wolf common-window ranking. The current text "DCA is closest to the bar at +1.93" is fine as a gate statement; "vol v3 moves #5 → #3" is the misuse — that's a magnitude comparison the DSR doesn't support. Effort: documentation only.

### R4 — Add a CPCV path for the highest-effort arcs (factor, relational)
For arcs with `n_trials > 30`, replace the single-split walk-forward with CPCV (6 groups, embargo = max(label_horizon, 5 days)). Each arc produces ~15 backtest paths instead of 1, and the DSR is computed on the *distribution* of paths rather than on the single chosen path. This is the López de Prado recommendation for ML-driven arcs. Effort: 3-5 days per arc; do factor first, it has the most trials. Reference: `apps/factor/scripts/modal/train_indicator.py::walkforward` already has the windowing scaffold.

### R5 — Family-level FDR correction
After R1–R3, the leaderboard has, say, 6 arcs that cleared the within-arc DSR gate. Apply **Benjamini–Hochberg FDR** at q=0.10 to the six p-values (`1 - DSR`). Tag arcs that survive as `family-FDR-cleared`. This is the Harvey–Liu 2020 recommendation. The cross-arc statement becomes: "k of n arcs cleared family-FDR-0.10 on the common window with Ledoit–Wolf significance vs passive DCA." Effort: 0.5 day.

### R6 — Regime-split every regime-conditional arc
For vol-v3 (regime-gated) and gate-v0 (overlay), report DSR separately on (regime-fired, regime-off) sub-streams using Pagan–Sossounov bull/bear dates. The arc is `confirmed-OOS` only if the regime-fired sub-stream clears DSR. Effort: 1-2 days.

### R7 — Log every trial in a per-arc `trial_log.jsonl`
Make `n_trials` an observed quantity, not a reconstructed one. Every config tested (including smoke tests, abandoned arms, hyperparameter sweeps) gets a JSONL row. The DSR `n_trials` is `len(trial_log)`. This is the López de Prado *Advances* protocol. Effort: 2 days; mostly building the wrapper, then retrofitting active arcs.

### What NOT to add
- **Don't add Jensen–Kelly–Pedersen hierarchical Bayes.** It needs ≥100 strategies clustered into themes. Six arcs isn't enough.
- **Don't add Romano–Wolf step-down across all 96 leaderboard rows.** Most rows aren't stream-bearing; the family is six, not 96.
- **Don't add a Sharpe-ratio efficient frontier (Bailey–López de Prado 2012b).** It's a portfolio-construction tool, not a comparison tool.
- **Don't ban DSR.** It's the correct gate. Just demote it from rank key.

## 7. Annotated bibliography (sorted by relevance to the ladder)

1. **Bailey & López de Prado 2014, "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio"** — the framework the ladder already uses. Selection-bias correction + non-normality adjustment. [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2460551)
2. **Bailey & López de Prado 2012, "The Sharpe Ratio Efficient Frontier"** — introduces PSR + MinTRL. The MinTRL formula is the strongest cross-arc-comparable scalar. [davidhbailey.com](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/sharpe-frontier.pdf)
3. **López de Prado 2018, *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*, chapter 7** — "Backtesting is not a research tool." The CPCV recipe. The protocol for `trial_log.jsonl`.
4. **Ledoit & Wolf 2008, "Robust Performance Hypothesis Testing with the Sharpe Ratio"** — the studentized stationary-bootstrap CI for ΔSR. **The cross-strategy ranking test the literature actually prescribes.** [ledoit.net/jef_2008pdf.pdf](http://www.ledoit.net/jef_2008pdf.pdf)
5. **Harvey, Liu & Zhu 2016, "...and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns"** — the t-hurdle = 3.0 result. Reframes multiple-testing in finance. [Duke PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Chapters/C38_Luck_versus_skill.pdf)
6. **Harvey & Liu 2020, "False (and Missed) Discoveries in Financial Economics"** — double bootstrap + BH-FDR for family-level correction. [Duke PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P143_False_and_missed.pdf)
7. **Hou, Xue & Zhang 2020, "Replicating Anomalies" (RFS)** — 65% of 452 anomalies fail |t|>1.96; 82% fail |t|>2.78. The empirical case for stricter t-hurdles. [NBER w23394](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23394/w23394.pdf)
8. **Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu 2014, "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting"** — CSCV → PBO. [SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2326253)
9. **Politis & Romano 1994, "The Stationary Bootstrap"** — the resampling primitive for Sharpe CIs. JASA 89:428. The block-length-randomized bootstrap that handles serial correlation.
10. **Hansen 2005, "A Test for Superior Predictive Ability"** — SPA, fixes power problems with White's RC. The N-strategy "is the best one real?" test.
11. **White 2000, "A Reality Check for Data Snooping"** — the foundational data-snooping correction. Econometrica 68(5):1097–1126.
12. **Sullivan, Timmermann & White 1999, "Data-Snooping, Technical Trading Rule Performance, and the Bootstrap"** — applies RC to ~7800 trading rules. JoF 54(5):1647–1691. The canonical "technical rules don't survive search-adjustment" finding.
13. **Romano & Wolf 2005, "Stepwise Multiple Testing as Formalized Data Snooping"** — FWER step-down. Econometrica 73(4):1237–1282.
14. **McLean & Pontiff 2016, "Does Academic Research Destroy Stock Return Predictability?"** — post-publication decay: 26% OOS, 58% post-pub. Sets the priors for `n_trials` and ex-ante decay. JoF 71(1):5–32.
15. **Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen 2023, "Is There a Replication Crisis in Finance?"** — hierarchical Bayes alternative to Harvey–Liu. JoF 78(5):2465–2518. The dissenting recent view. [NBER w28432](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28432)
16. **Daniel & Moskowitz 2016, "Momentum Crashes"** — regime-conditional risk management. JFE 122:221–247. The model for how to honestly evaluate a regime-conditional arc.
17. **Pagan & Sossounov 2003, "A Simple Framework for Analyzing Bull and Bear Markets"** — formal regime-dating rules. J. Applied Econometrics. The recommended pre-registered regime split.
18. **Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang & Yan 2020, "On the performance of volatility-managed portfolios"** — Moreira–Muir doesn't survive OOS. JFE. The cautionary tale for single-overlay regime arcs.
19. **DeMiguel, Martin-Utrera & Uppal 2024, "A Multifactor Perspective on Volatility-Managed Portfolios"** — multifactor conditional strategy survives OOS. JoF 79(1). The response to Cederburg et al.
20. **Bessembinder 2018, "Do Stocks Outperform Treasury Bills?"** — 4.3% of stocks drive all excess returns. JFE 129:440–457. The skew-and-long-tail methodology lesson for long-horizon comparisons.
21. **Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen 2013, "Value and Momentum Everywhere"** — the canonical cross-asset multi-strategy comparison: aligns 8 markets on common history. JoF 68(3):929–985. The implicit prescription for common-window alignment.

## 8. Headline recommendation

**Adopt R1 — common-window Ledoit–Wolf Sharpe-difference CIs — as the new primary cross-arc ranking key, with DSR demoted to a within-arc selection-bias gate.** This single change converts the ladder from "ranked by a within-arc statistic that doesn't claim cross-arc comparability" into "ranked by the field's canonical pairwise-Sharpe-difference test on the apples-to-apples date intersection." MinTRL (R2) is a useful second scalar column. R3 is documentation only and should ship simultaneously. R4 (CPCV) is the right longer-horizon investment for the factor arc specifically. The current page's text "DCA is closest to the bar at +1.93" is a correct gate-statement and can stand; the magnitude comparisons between arcs need to be replaced with Ledoit–Wolf-pairwise statements.
