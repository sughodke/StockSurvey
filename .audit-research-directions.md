# Audit — what the StockSurvey research may have gotten wrong about itself

A fresh read of `apps/docs/docs/**` ignoring CLAUDE.md's "operational rules" as priors. The verdicts below treat each finding as a fresh hypothesis. The TL;DR is that the work is *technically careful* (precision bugs are tracked, costs are at least gestured at, walk-forwards are real), but several of the "the answer is X" closures are load-bearing on choices that look ad hoc in hindsight and that, if relaxed, plausibly flip the verdict.

## Summary — 5 strongest candidates for "this is what was actually missed"

1. **The CFR Phase 4d close-out is structurally underpowered.** The final-swing `cfr-macro-gate-final.md` declares the bot dead on a "positive in only 1/5 windows" criterion — but in 4 of those 5 windows the gate was *closed*, so the experiment by construction substituted EW for CFR and alpha is exactly 0.000. The denominator should be "windows where the gate fired", giving 1/1. Worse, the gate held closed in w0 (CFR raw +1.367 vs EW +0.945 → would have been +0.42 alpha) and w3 (CFR raw +0.78 vs EW +0.69 → +0.09) — the gate misclassified two of CFR's three positive-alpha windows. The "memory-heavy gate" diagnosis is a coherent post-hoc story, but the test that was actually run can't reject "the gate is the wrong gate" vs "the bot is dead". A shorter-lookback gate (30-90d) or a percentile gate is one experiment away from being run and was never tried.
2. **The friction model that killed CFR is single-point and includes a circular labor term.** `cfr-vs-dca-realistic.md` uses 50 bps/yr for CFR vs 5 bps/yr for DCA, then explicitly counts "~30 min/month operator labor at $50/hr" against the bot — but the bot's whole point is to be a *passive script invoked by cron*. The 50 bps figure is reverse-engineered from a turnover assumption (5.9× annual × 5 bps + 15-20 bps tax + labor). No sensitivity table. At 25 bps drag (still pessimistic; commission_bps=10 is already in the backtest!) the realistic alpha is **+0.045**, comfortably above the +0.015 net figure that closed the arc. The whole canonical-strategy switch hangs on one un-bracketed assumption.
3. **`apps/pairs` was filed `confirmed-null` because of one bad window (w0), without testing the obvious cheap pre-screen.** Mean ex-w0 is +0.365 across 5 windows (passes the marginal threshold) and the dot-com cointegration that killed w0 is a 2000-2005 train slice; a `min_train_year ≥ 2003` test (free, ~10 min wall) would isolate it. The EG-passing-rate "natural regime indicator" was identified *in the finding itself* but never tested as a screen. Pair trading is plausibly deployable with a sample-size or recency filter on the cointegrating relationships.
4. **The "passive EW benchmark" reframes everything but is computed against an `ffill-and-drop` ghost universe that holds delisted names at last price.** The doc itself flags this bias as "~0.05-0.15 Sharpe optimistic" for passive (more on broader universes). That single caveat is the difference between several models being "alpha-negative" and being "within noise". The bias is asymmetric: it inflates passive Sharpe (no rebalancing cost on a name that vanished) but doesn't inflate model Sharpe (model rebals out of the bad name on schedule). A cleanly-handled delisting accounting could flip the ex-Phase-2 −0.334 to within −0.20, and possibly flip stooq_us_long's −0.133 Morlet result entirely.
5. **No `apps/vol` v1 was ever run despite the 5/5 directional consistency.** The v0 result has the cleanest "real signal" pattern in the entire repo (mean val Pearson r +0.12 multivariate, 5/5 positive alpha windows, late windows posting val r > 0.23). The follow-ups identified by the finding itself — per-rebal portfolio aggregation, costs-in-the-loop, MLP head, DoltHub extension to 2026 — were all parked "until we revisit", and were never revisited because the pivot moved to CFR. This is the single highest-EV unexamined branch in the repo.

## Per-arc critique

### Regime (`apps/regime`)

**Stated conclusion:** Optuna best-params bounce window-to-window; JAX-Adam scale weights collapse to long horizons (126d 48%); raw close beats log-returns CWT input; signal is monthly-to-biannual.

**Alternative interpretation they didn't engage:** The baseline `lookback=120, n_tail=20, top_n=20, KL divergence` gets Sharpe **0.63** vs equal-weight Sharpe **1.26** on the same window — i.e. the regime app is alpha-NEGATIVE vs EW from the very first eval in the docs. Every later regime improvement is measured against the +0.63 baseline, not against the +1.26 EW. The "Optuna instability" diagnostic and the JAX-Adam collapse are both *within an alpha-negative regime*. Nothing in the arc tests whether any regime variant clears EW. The `passive-ew-benchmark` finding called this out for relational but never extended the audit to regime, which is bizarre because regime's own baselines page has the same arithmetic visible.

**Cheapest follow-up:** rerun `weights_regime` on `phase-2` and `stooq_us_long` at default params and compute EW alpha. ~15 min. If alpha is negative on both, regime as a whole is confirmed alpha-negative and the CWT-portfolio approach has been quietly falsified.

**Severity:** medium. The CWT scoring infrastructure is reused everywhere (relational, replay, factor) — if regime's own baseline is alpha-negative, the prior on CWT-derived strategies should be lower than the repo's tone implies.

### Relational (`apps/relational`)

**Stated conclusion:** Phase-2 wins are mega-cap-specific; wider-universe Morlet bundle is "less-bad-loss" vs EW. kNN-on-CWT-fingerprint strategy class is probably structurally wrong. Fingerprint is OK for selection/timing, fails as hedge.

**Alternative interpretation:** The "passive EW benchmark" comparison that downgraded the relational arc has a **delisting-handling bug** that the finding itself admits ("~0.05-0.15 Sharpe optimistic for passive"). `pd.DataFrame.ffill().dropna(axis=1)` holds delisted names flat at last price. That's not a passive-investor experience — a real passive investor's allocation gets sold at the last trading price and reallocated. The ffill convention essentially gives passive a free "no cost to dispose of bankruptcy" pass. The 312-name `stooq_us_long` over 12 years has non-trivial delistings; the 2162-name factor-wide has many more. Crucially: this bias is **monotone with universe breadth**, exactly matching the "monotonic passive-Sharpe decay" pattern the finding celebrates. Some unknown fraction of the "wider universe → lower passive bar → easier to beat" structural claim is actually "wider universe → more delistings → more ffill bias inflating passive". A cleaner accounting (price = NaN after delisting → mark to zero in the basket → drop weight + rebalance) could move the ex-Phase-2 EW from +0.832 down to ~+0.65-0.75, which would put the Morlet result (val +0.717) at +0 to −0.05 alpha — within noise rather than catastrophic.

**Cheapest follow-up:** Re-run `equal_weight_benchmark.py` with a delisting-aware basket that marks-to-zero on the next NaN and rebalances surviving constituents. Compare passive Sharpe at delta. ~1 hour wall.

**Severity:** high. This single bias choice may be propagating into 4+ "alpha vs EW" verdicts across the repo, including the CFR Phase 4d realistic-friction comparison.

### Factor (`apps/factor`)

**Stated conclusion:** Cross-sectional return prediction is signal-bound at val IC +0.005-0.012; long-short / loss-pivot / aux-head all confirmed-null; rank-IC's spread-thin behavior was inadvertent shrinkage that worked. Pivot to a different prediction problem.

**Alternative interpretations:**

1. **The horizon was never honestly searched.** The "horizon pivot rebal=63d" row went *quarterly* (`reversed-OOS`) without trying the natural alternative direction: **shorter than 20d**. Cross-sectional return signal at 5-10 days is well-studied in the literature (1-week reversal, gap-and-go, post-earnings drift) — but `apps/factor`'s entire walk-forward shape is hardcoded at `rebal_days=20`. A val IC of +0.005 at 20d may be +0.015-0.025 at 5-10d, which crosses the long-short friction floor cleanly. The `rebal-days-sweep` TODO exists but only mentions DWT-L1; the factor head sweep was never tried.
2. **The "loss-pivot" was tested on the wrong head.** The block_sharpe and ir_vs_ew losses converged to log-temperature ~0.2 (near-argmax) on a head trained on a +0.005 IC signal. Of course that destroys val Sharpe — at +0.005 IC, the optimum has to be a long *broad* basket, not concentrated picks. The right test is "does block_sharpe loss work when the head has +0.02-0.05 train IC"? The indicator-grid head at +0.012 val IC is the *low end* of the eligible range. A higher-capacity backbone (Replay CNN) or a higher-edge feature class (factor-narrow's industry-relative-momentum which isn't in the indicator grid) is the test that was skipped.

**Cheapest follow-up:** Rerun `train_scorer_walkforward` at `rebal_days=5` and `rebal_days=10` on the existing indicator grid head, factor-narrow. ~30 min wall on Modal. If val IC at 5d is ≥ +0.02, the entire confirmed-null on long-short/loss-pivot needs to be re-litigated.

**Severity:** high. The "cross-sectional return prediction is exhausted" conclusion drove the entire prediction-problem-pivot arc; if 5d shows materially more signal, the whole pivot was premature.

### Replay (`apps/replay`)

**Stated conclusion:** DWT-L1 keep-LL preserves indicator reconstruction at 4× shrink; length-axis K was over-provisioned; MACD reconstruction is pathological. Backbone produces npz artifacts factor consumes.

**Alternative interpretation:** Replay's R² metric is *target-reconstruction quality*, not portfolio quality. The "factor SSL walkforward doesn't clear indicator baseline" row in the leaderboard quietly establishes that the replay-pretrained backbone *underperforms* the deterministic indicator stack downstream (val IC +0.003 SSL vs +0.012 indicator). That's a brutal result for the entire SSL-pretraining workstream, but it lives in the factor app's finding pages, not replay's. **The replay arc never closed the loop**: "we made the encoder reconstruct indicators well — does that pretraining lift downstream factor IC?" Answer (from factor side): no, it lowers it. But the replay docs read as if the work is succeeding. There is no `findings/replay-doesnt-help-factor.md` synthesis page.

**Cheapest follow-up:** Just write the synthesis page. The data exists; the conclusion is just missing as an explicit findings doc.

**Severity:** medium-low. The replay infrastructure is preserved as a useful artifact regardless; the missing synthesis is documentation hygiene, not a new experiment.

### Gate (`apps/gate`)

**Stated conclusion:** Drawdown forecasting has real signal (val Pearson r +0.26, 25-50× factor IC) but binary gate is fragile at v0; partial-OOS. Mean alpha +0.067 with 4/6 positive windows.

**Alternative interpretation:** Three things look off here:

1. **Costs not in v0.** The finding admits "gate flips 10-40 times per 780-bar val window at 10 bps round-trip = 0.13-0.5%/yr friction". At 0.5%/yr, on a 13% vol equity basket, that's ~0.04 Sharpe per window — close to wiping out the +0.067 mean alpha. The verdict is "real signal, marginal Sharpe" but with friction it's likely "real signal, indistinguishable from zero". The v1 follow-up to add costs in the loop was never run.
2. **Sharpe is the wrong metric.** A drawdown gate's *job* is to reduce CVaR / max DD, not to lift Sharpe. The finding never reports the metric the gate is actually designed to improve: did the gate reduce max DD? Did Sortino move? CVaR? Window 1's "+0.32 alpha by flatting to 51% exposure during GFC" is exactly the kind of thing where Sharpe is a bad summary because it averages the volatility-of-returns over the whole window including the avoided crash. A gate that prevents a -30% drawdown but costs 0.2 Sharpe in calm periods may be Sharpe-flat but CVaR-positive and totally deployable as a risk overlay.
3. **The v1a result is misread.** The COVID window kill is blamed on "macro features have non-stationary distributions across train/val". That's a real mechanism, but the actual diagnosis path — *the model trained on 2015-2020 Goldilocks predicted high VIX → DD on the post-COVID recovery* — is a textbook regularization problem, not a problem with macro features per se. Standard fix: ridge / shrinkage / cross-validated regularization strength on the macro features. None tried. The "confirmed-null worse than baseline" stamp on v1a is harsher than the evidence supports.

**Cheapest follow-up:** Recompute gate v0 metrics with `max_dd`, `cvar_05`, `sortino` reported alongside Sharpe; also redo v1a with ridge regression instead of OLS on the macro features. ~2 hours.

**Severity:** medium. The gate looks more deployable than the verdict label suggests if you change the metric.

### Pairs (`apps/pairs`)

**Stated conclusion:** `confirmed-null` per pre-reg (mean +0.099). Regime-conditional signal (works 2008-2017, fails in bull markets). Park; pivot to vol.

**Alternative interpretation:** The single bad window (w0, −1.23) drags the mean. Mean ex-w0 is +0.365 — passes the marginal threshold. The finding itself documents:

- w0 trains on **2000-2005** (the dot-com cointegration period, structurally different from any later regime)
- EG-passing-rate per window is **itself a regime indicator** (3522-4755 in "working" windows, 2249-2857 in "failing" later windows)

Two follow-ups identified in the finding, neither tried:

1. **Min-train-year filter.** Skip training slices that begin before some cutoff (e.g. 2003). Free to test, would isolate w0's effect.
2. **EG-passing-rate regime gate.** Deploy only when train EG-passing-rate exceeds a threshold. The finding flagged this as "v2 follow-up, risk of overfitting threshold to this dataset" — but the threshold is one-dimensional and the discrimination is large (>4000 = good, <3000 = mixed). A reasonable pre-registered cut on a 5y rolling EG-passing-rate would not be overfit to the 6 windows under test.

The `confirmed-null` verdict is strictly correct by the pre-registered cut. The *operational call* "pair trading doesn't work" is wrong; it should be "unconditional pair trading on factor-narrow with no regime filter doesn't work, and we never tested with a filter".

**Cheapest follow-up:** Add an EG-passing-rate gate, pre-register it (deploy only in windows where train EG > 3500), re-run. ~10 min wall (the screening is the slow part, and it's already done — this is just a per-window filter). If alpha goes from +0.099 → ~+0.5 with a sensibly pre-registered threshold, pair trading flips to deployable.

**Severity:** high. This was the second `confirmed-null` in the prediction-problem-pivot arc and helped drive the "stop testing variations, pivot to vol" decision. If pairs is actually `partial-OOS` with an obvious regime gate, the pivot to vol was premature and the CFR arc maybe doesn't get prioritized for two weeks.

### Vol (`apps/vol`)

**Stated conclusion:** `inconclusive`. Multivariate val r +0.12 is real and 40× the univariate signal. 5/5 positive windows is the cleanest directional consistency in the pivot arc. Per-cell Sharpe alpha +0.089 just below +0.10 marginal floor.

**Alternative interpretation:** This was the strongest result in the entire repo and got benched. Five issues:

1. **The metric was per-cell Sharpe, which the finding itself calls "a weak metric"** — no temporal aggregation, no annualization. The honest portfolio Sharpe over per-rebal aggregated PnL was never computed. The +0.089 alpha number is on the worst-case interpretation; a real portfolio-level Sharpe might be larger or smaller, and they didn't measure.
2. **Costs not modeled, and options friction is 100-1000 bps round-trip per the doc.** This is a serious blocker but exactly the kind of thing that flips `inconclusive` to a clear verdict — was never measured.
3. **Universe was 3,877 SPX symbols including thin-option names.** The v1 list mentions "restrict to top-100 OI per date" — would dramatically improve the deployability story without losing the cross-sectional signal.
4. **DoltHub extension was identified as the v1 OOS check.** Late windows (2022-12 → 2023-06) carry the signal; the natural test is whether 2023-2026 (which DoltHub covers, gauss314 doesn't) continues to. This is OOS extension, not retraining — should be ~1 day of wall to wire up.
5. **MLP head was identified but never tested**, despite the v0 train R² being non-zero in late windows (suggesting nonlinear structure to exploit).

**Cheapest follow-up:** Run the per-rebal portfolio aggregator. That alone resolves the metric problem and produces a real portfolio Sharpe. ~4 hours wall.

**Severity:** very high. This is the most likely candidate to be a deployable strategy that was abandoned because the pivot arc moved on.

### CFR (`apps/cfr`)

**Stated conclusion:** 10 phase variants tested. Multi-asset Phase 4d the only PASS on raw alpha (+0.056 vs EW). Realistic friction kills it (net +0.015). Macro gate fails on positive-window count (1/5). DCA wins; CFR arc fully closed.

**Alternative interpretations (this is the biggest pile):**

1. **The friction model is undocumented and pessimistic.** `CFR_DRAG_BPS_YR = 50.0` is hardcoded; the breakdown in `cfr-vs-dca-realistic.md` decomposes it as 30 bps slippage + 15-20 bps short-term tax + operational labor. But the backtest *already* charged 10 bps commission per rebal — is that included in the 50 bps or additive? The doc says "Both sides start from the 10 bps commission already in the backtest" then adds 50 bps for CFR and 5 bps for DCA — which implies the *total* drag on CFR is ~10 + 50 = 60 bps/yr while DCA is ~10 + 5 = 15 bps/yr. The 5 bps DCA number is suspicious: DCA rebals quarterly at 5% drift on 13 names, so turnover ≈ 50%/yr × 5 bps spread ≈ 2.5 bps; plus 10 bps commission on actual rebal events (~4-8/yr × ~5% drift triggers × 13 assets) = also a few bps. Where the 50 bps for CFR comes from breakdown:
   - 30 bps from "5 bps slippage × 5.9× annual one-way turnover ≈ 30 bps" — but 5 bps is high for liquid ETFs like TLT/GLD (1-2 bps is typical)
   - 15-20 bps from "short-term tax inefficiency" — but the bot can be operated in a tax-advantaged account (IRA, 401k), zeroing this
   - operational labor — but the whole rationale for an autonomous bot is that labor is amortized; charging it implies the bot doesn't deliver on its own thesis
   At 25 bps drag (slippage only, 2 bps × 11× one-way × no tax × no labor), realistic CFR alpha is **+0.045** vs +0.015 reported, a comfortable above-noise number. The choice of 50 bps is what closed the arc; no sensitivity table was produced.
2. **The window-level VIX gate test is malformed.** The `cfr-macro-gate-final.md` pre-reg cut FAIL = "alpha ≤ 0 OR positive in ≤ 2/5". The result was alpha +0.053 (positive, in the MARGINAL band) — the FAIL was triggered solely by "positive in 1/5". But in 4/5 windows, the gate was CLOSED → bot replaced by EW → alpha is *exactly zero by construction* (CFR Phase 4d's per-window alpha was never tested against DCA in those 4 windows because they didn't deploy). The 1/5 count is "windows where gate fired AND CFR beat DCA-with-friction". Of the 1 window where the gate fired, 1/1 was positive. The pre-reg cut conflates "did the gate fire" with "was the bot net positive when deployed" — these are different questions. With a denominator of "windows where the gate fired", the verdict is 1/1 positive, which is statistically meaningless but not a FAIL.
3. **The gate misclassified two of CFR's three best windows.** Per the actual eval JSON (`Output/cfr-phase4d-vix-gated.json`):
   - w0: VIX 19.3 vs median 25.4 → CLOSED. CFR raw +1.367 vs EW +0.945 → +0.42 alpha thrown away.
   - w3: VIX 15.2 vs median 15.4 → CLOSED. CFR raw +0.78 vs EW +0.69 → +0.09 alpha thrown away.
   - w4: VIX 23.0 vs median 21.9 → OPEN. CFR raw +1.04 vs EW +0.74 → +0.30 alpha captured.

   The gate fires when VIX is *above its trailing median* — i.e. when vol is high. But CFR's edge comes from cross-asset rotation, which works when *dispersion across asset classes* is high. These are correlated but distinct. The "memory-heavy gate" diagnosis is a coherent post-hoc rationalization for w0 specifically, but the gate is *also* wrong in w3 (a near-tie threshold where VIX 15.2 vs median 15.4 is essentially indistinguishable). One alternative gate that would have fired in all 3 winning windows: trailing 60d cross-asset dispersion above median (computed within CFR's own universe). This is a one-experiment test that was never run.
4. **The "binding constraint is signal availability, not architecture" claim is over-strong.** The CFR Phase 1 → 3 progression was +0.021 mean Sharpe (tabular → deep). That's small but the doc treats it as bounded by no-regret theory. But the deep arch *did* lift w2 from −0.111 to +0.127 alpha — a +0.24 swing on a single window. The aggregate +0.021 is dragged by the deep arch hurting other windows. The story "deep architecture didn't help" is not the same as "in some regimes the deep architecture helps a lot and in others it hurts" — the latter is what the per-window data shows, and would naturally suggest "ensemble or regime-gate the architecture choice", which was never tried.
5. **`naive uniform mix` is the wrong baseline for the "menu enrichment makes it worse" claim.** Phase 1→2a→2b shows naive uniform rising +0.591 → +0.632 → +0.652 — which the doc reads as "uniform mix gets better with more actions". But it's also consistent with "the new actions are individually alpha-positive; their addition lifts ANY mix containing them; CFR doesn't lift faster because it has to learn to weight them which takes more samples". The right baseline for "is CFR learning what it should" isn't naive uniform, it's "CFR with infinite training data on the same menu" — i.e. the supervised optimal in-hindsight portfolio. That comparison was never run.

**Cheapest follow-up (in priority order):**

1. Sensitivity-table the CFR friction at {15, 25, 35, 50} bps/yr and DCA at {2, 5, 10}. ~1 hour. If the verdict flips at 30 bps CFR / 5 bps DCA, the arc is not closed; it's parameter-dependent.
2. Re-run the macro gate test with a 60d or 90d rolling-median VIX cutoff. ~30 min wall. If the 60d gate fires in w0, w3, w4 (the three positive-alpha windows), the "memory-heavy gates fight regime transitions" rule is correct, the gate is fixable, and CFR is back on the table.
3. Add an in-CFR-universe cross-asset dispersion gate (rolling 60d of average pairwise correlation among 13 assets). Same cost; uses no external data.

**Severity:** critical. The CFR closure is the single biggest pivot in the recent research and it rests on:
- one unbracketed friction number (50 bps);
- one pre-reg cut that conflates "gate fired" with "deployment was profitable";
- one rolling-median lookback (1y) that the finding itself partly admits is wrong.

If any of those three is fragile, DCA-vs-CFR is not settled.

### DCA (`apps/dca`)

**Stated conclusion:** Canonical live strategy as of 2026-05-13. 13-asset EW basket, quarterly rebal, 5 risk rails. Sharpe +0.673 full-panel, CAGR +8.4%, max DD −40.7% (GFC).

**Alternative interpretation:** DCA is a fine default. But it inherits all the framing issues from the CFR closure:

1. **DCA's victory is a function of the friction asymmetry.** At 5 bps drag for DCA vs 50 for CFR, DCA wins by +0.21 on worst-window Sharpe. At equal friction (impossible for CFR but a reasonable lower bound) the gap shrinks.
2. **The basket was never sanity-checked vs Bridgewater All Weather's own published weights or vs 60/40.** "Multi-asset EW DCA" is one of an infinite family of multi-asset DCA strategies; the doc admits "post-Volcker monetary regime bias-corrected Sharpe is closer to +0.55" but never benchmarks against the standard alternatives. A user reading `apps/dca.md` could reasonably conclude they should run this; but a 60/40 SPY/AGG has Sharpe ~0.75 and zero operational cost.
3. **The 5% drift threshold and 80-day cadence floor were chosen by ergonomics, not optimization.** No sweep over these parameters in the docs. If quarterly is right because "in-kind redemption is tax-efficient at quarter ends", that's a soft rationale; if it's 80 trading days because "that's about a quarter", that's a heuristic. Worth at least one sensitivity sweep before pinning as canonical.

**Cheapest follow-up:** Add 60/40 SPY/AGG and Risk-Parity (e.g. naive volatility-weighted across the same 13 assets) to the DCA benchmark page. ~1 hour wall.

**Severity:** medium. DCA is probably *fine* in absolute terms but isn't sharply justified as the *best* simple alternative.

## Cross-cutting themes

### Theme 1: pre-registered cuts get used as binary go/no-go without sensitivity to threshold choice

`pairs` is the clearest example: mean +0.099 fails "≥ +0.20 marginal" by 0.10, which is well within bootstrap noise on 6 windows. `vol` is +0.089 vs +0.10 marginal, off by 0.01. The macro-gate-final is +0.053 alpha (positive, MARGINAL band) flipped to FAIL by the "positive in 1/5" criterion, which is computed on a denominator that doesn't make sense. Across the leaderboard, several verdicts are within one standard error of flipping; the labels treat them as decisive. The verdict-label vocabulary is *append-only* — but the operational rule "stop testing this lever, pivot" follows from the label, not from a quantitative re-examination.

### Theme 2: a "ffill delisted prices to last close" convention is silently load-bearing

The passive-EW benchmark uses `pd.DataFrame.ffill().dropna(axis=1)`. The finding flags this as `~0.05-0.15 Sharpe optimistic for passive`. That bias is monotone with universe breadth (more delistings on wider universes), exactly matching the celebrated "passive Sharpe decays monotonically as universe broadens" pattern. The bias is also model-asymmetric: a model that rebals out of bad names doesn't get the free ride; passive does. The "alpha vs EW" column on every wide-universe row may be biased downward by ~0.1 Sharpe. Across the relational arc, this is 2-3 verdicts; across the CFR arc, it propagates into the DCA-wins-on-worst-window claim.

### Theme 3: the "confirmed-null → pivot to a different prediction problem" rule moves too fast

The verdict-action table says `confirmed-null` triggers "stop testing variations of the same lever — find an orthogonal one". In practice this has been applied after **one or two variant tests**. Examples:

- `factor` got 3 nulls (passive-EW, long-short, loss-pivot) and pivoted; never tried rebal_days < 20.
- `pairs` got 1 null and pivoted; never tried the EG-passing-rate gate the finding identified.
- `gate v1a` got 1 null and the macro-as-direct-features path was abandoned; never tried ridge regression.

The rule is good in principle (avoids endless local search) but in practice the "orthogonal lever" pivot has produced a long arc of partial-OOS results (gate / pairs / vol / cfr) that all share the same regime-conditional failure mode. The lateral move didn't escape the binding constraint; iterating in place might have.

### Theme 4: the metric is mostly Sharpe, even when the question is not Sharpe

- `gate` is supposed to reduce DD; reports Sharpe.
- `pairs` is intrinsically long-short / vol-of-spread; reports Sharpe vs a zero benchmark (correctly), but never measures CVaR or stress-period CAGR.
- `vol-surface` was tested with per-cell Sharpe, which the doc itself flags as "a weak metric".
- `regime` baseline (Sharpe 0.63 vs EW 1.26) gets celebrated as "high return" but the entire framing assumes Sharpe-maximization.

The repo doesn't have a `CVaR_05`, `max_DD`, or `Sortino` column on the leaderboard. For strategies whose value proposition is tail behavior (drawdown gates, defensive overlays), this is the wrong primary metric.

### Theme 5: window choice — the 6-window walk-forward has structural adversarial windows

Multiple findings (gate, pairs, cfr-phase4) post their only positive-alpha windows in 2008 GFC, 2010-2013 post-GFC recovery, 2022 post-Fed-pivot. Multiple findings post catastrophic single-window losses in 2005-2007 (mid-bull) and 2016-2019 ("everything works passive"). The 6-window protocol's `train=1260 / val=780 / step=780` choice means each window has a fairly fixed regime alignment. A `train=1260 / val=252 / step=252` (5 windows but 10 years of val coverage with finer regime resolution) is a different sample; nobody ran it. The leaderboard's own reading note 4 admits "partial-OOS usually means 1 outlier window inflates the mean" — but the response has been "stratify and reconsider", not "test if a different windowing protocol gives different verdicts".

## Closing observation

The research is meticulous about *recording* what it tried; less meticulous about *what the verdict implies about the next experiment*. The verdict-label vocabulary is excellent for forcing OOS thinking, but the "default-next question" lookup table has been treated as procedural (one pre-reg → one verdict → one pivot direction) rather than as a starting point for the actual decision. In a research environment where the cheapest experiments cost an hour and the wrong-direction pivots cost weeks, the bias toward pivot-on-first-null has produced a thicket of partial-OOS results that share the same regime-conditional structure — and the diagnosis "find a regime filter" is itself partly a victim of that bias, because the regime filter that was eventually tried (window-level VIX-above-1y-median) is one of many candidates and the wrong one's failure shouldn't close out the strategy class. The CFR-vs-DCA close-out is the most consequential decision in this body of work; it is materially under-stress-tested.
