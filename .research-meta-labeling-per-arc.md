# Meta-labeling per arc — research brief

**Date:** 2026-05-24
**Scope:** Should we pre-register and run López de Prado-style meta-labeling
per arc as the next allocation experiment, or has the question already been
answered?
**TL;DR up front:** The exact protocol the user is considering — one binary
classifier per arc on "will-this-arc-earn-its-Sharpe-over-H-days," features
= `ss_macro` 6-stack + arc-internal trailing Sharpe/vol — was **already
pre-registered and run on 2026-05-23 as candidate C3 in the meta-allocator
walk-forward** and landed `reversed-OOS` against both 1/N (B2) and
inverse-arc-vol (B3). Pooled OOS Sharpe **+1.068** vs B3 **+1.722**; ΔSR_ann
**−0.655** [−1.138, −0.145]. The protocol does not need re-running; it needs
deprioritizing in favor of (a) — a locked inverse-arc-vol robustness check
without vol_v3 — or, if the user wants a *genuinely* new lever, (b) — the
Kritzman-Page-Turkington K=2 turbulence gate applied as a *risk-off scalar*
only, not as an arc selector. Recommendation at the bottom.

## 1. Does the protocol map cleanly to our setup?

**No — and the mismatch is the load-bearing issue.** Canonical
López de Prado meta-labeling (AFML Ch. 3, Wiley 2018; Singh & Joubert
2022, *J. Financial Data Science*, Hudson-Thames whitepaper "Does Meta
Labeling Add to Signal Efficacy?") is a **two-stage primary-secondary
pipeline on a single instrument and a single primary signal**:

1. **Primary model** emits a directional signal (e.g. SMA-crossover say
   "buy now"). Labels generated via the *triple-barrier method* — for
   each event, the label is +1/0/−1 depending on which of (take-profit,
   stop-loss, time-out) hits first.
2. **Secondary (meta) model** is a binary classifier on
   `{should-act-on-this-primary-signal: 0/1}`. Features are the primary
   signal's strength plus side information. The output **scales position
   size** by predicted probability; the primary model continues to
   provide the *direction*.

Singh-Joubert (2022) test exactly this — one ticker (S&P 500 E-mini), two
primary strategies (Bollinger mean-reversion, SMA trend), and the secondary
classifier is used **for sizing the same primary signal**, not for choosing
between strategies. Their reported lift is in classification metrics
(precision Bollinger 0.17 → 0.20; SMA 0.48 → 0.54) translated into Sharpe
improvements they describe qualitatively. They do not benchmark against
inverse-vol mixing, persistence, or 1/N. They do not test cross-strategy
selection.

The user's framing — *N independent classifiers, one per arc, predicting
arc-Sharpe-state* — is **not meta-labeling in the López de Prado sense**.
It is "N independent return-state classifiers stacked with a soft-voting
allocator." That's a perfectly reasonable thing to try, but:

- It loses the primary/secondary structural pairing that gives canonical
  meta-labeling its power (the secondary corrects errors of a specific
  primary).
- It loses the triple-barrier label, which is path-dependent and event-
  driven. The user's substitute label ("forward-H Sharpe ≥ full-sample
  Sharpe") is path-independent and threshold-arbitrary.
- It inherits the *N-way selection-bias problem* that triple-barrier
  meta-labeling does not have — every arc gets its own classifier,
  every classifier sees the same macro features, and the allocator's
  job is genuinely cross-sectional.

A more honest framing would be "stacked binary regime-state predictors
combined by soft vote" or "per-strategy macro-conditional gating." Either
way the protocol's relevant ancestors are not AFML Ch. 3 but **Rapach-
Strauss-Zhou 2010** (combination forecasts) and the **Pesaran-Timmermann
2007** literature on selection-of-forecasters — both of which the repo's
literature brief already cites as confirmed-null on cross-sectional equity
return forecasts at horizons < 1 year.

**Bottom line for Q1:** what's being called "meta-labeling per arc" is a
loose analogy, not an instance of the published protocol. The published
protocol (single primary, triple-barrier, secondary sizing) does not
directly apply to our 6-arc selection problem at all.

## 2. Label horizon and threshold

Already locked at **H = 63 days, threshold = arc's full-sample Sharpe** in
the 2026-05-23 pre-reg (TODO/meta-allocator-regime-forecasting.md line 130-
136). The choice was reasonable:

- **H = 21d** is too short — arc Sharpes are dominated by daily-return
  noise at that horizon; the label flips roughly half the time and the
  classifier learns nothing.
- **H = 126d** halves the effective n of labels and stretches the
  train/val boundary leakage zone — at expanding-window training on a
  rebal-every-20-day cadence, a 126-day forward label overlaps the next
  6.3 rebal points. Triple-barrier addresses this with sample-uniqueness
  weights (`avg_uniqueness` in AFML §4.5); a plain logistic does not.
- **H = 63d** sits at the dca / regime-arc Sharpe estimation floor
  (Lo 2002 *Statistics of Sharpe Ratios* — at σ_annual ≈ 14%, ann-Sharpe
  s.e. for n = 63 daily obs is ≈ √(1/63) ≈ 0.126; the label
  signal-to-noise is at best ≈ 1× per arc per rebal). That's the bar.

The **label-leakage problem the pre-reg did not fully address**: at H = 63
trading days and a 20 TD rebal cadence, every training example
overlaps the next 3.15 examples. AFML §4.5 deals with this via observation-
uniqueness weighting; the C3 implementation does not (per the script
`apps/docs/scripts/meta_allocator_run.py` — confirm before re-running).
This is one place a meta-labeling re-run *could* show improvement, but the
expected magnitude is small (Singh-Joubert report ≈ 5-10% precision gains
from uniqueness weighting on their single-instrument data).

The threshold "full-sample Sharpe" is the **biggest unforced source of
label bias**: it uses test-set information to define the in-sample target.
A correct walk-forward would use "trailing 252d Sharpe at train-end" or
"trailing expanding-window Sharpe up to train-end" as the per-arc
threshold. The 2026-05-23 C3 run used full-sample. **If anyone re-runs the
protocol the threshold needs to be re-cast as point-in-time first.** This
is the strongest pro-replay argument I can make for C3.

For our n=46-regime sample (regimes ≥ 21 TD since 2005, from
`Output/regimes-since-2005.json`), the H=63d label is binary across
≈ (5300 trading days − 252 warmup) / 20 ≈ 252 rebal points per arc, ×
6 arcs (vol_v3 only post-2024) ≈ 1100 labeled obs total. With 9 features
that's 122 obs/feature — above the 10-per-parameter folklore floor but
well below the 50-per-feature regime where logistic regression starts to
show stable OOS calibration (Peduzzi 1996 in epidemiology; van Smeden et
al. 2019 in *Stat Med* updates this to ~20 events-per-variable for binary
outcomes).

## 3. Feature stack and circularity risk

The pre-reg's C3 used 9 features: `ss_macro` 6-stack (fed_funds, slope_10y_3m,
credit_baa, m2_yoy, real_yield_10y, vix) + arc-own trailing-63d Sharpe +
arc-own trailing-252d Sharpe + arc-own trailing-63d realized vol.

**Circularity risk is substantial but bounded:**

- The label is "forward-63d Sharpe ≥ full-sample Sharpe."
- Feature 7 is trailing-63d Sharpe.
- The correlation of trailing-63d Sharpe with forward-63d Sharpe is
  bounded above by the Sharpe-autocorrelation, which in the
  Moskowitz-Ooi-Pedersen 2012 paper is ≈ +0.15 at 1-quarter lag for
  TSMOM strategies, and decays to noise by 1-year lag.
- So feature 7 *can* legitimately predict the label at low magnitude
  without being a leakage proxy. But it carries a substantial fraction
  of all the predictive content the classifier will find — meaning the
  multi-feature classifier is mostly a noisy reimplementation of "buy
  the arc with the high trailing-Sharpe" (persistence) **plus** noise
  from the 6 macro features.

This is the **mechanism by which C3 underperformed both inverse-vol and
persistence**: the trailing-Sharpe feature carries the persistence signal,
the macro features add noise, and the soft-vote across 6 fitted classifiers
re-introduces selection cost. The repo finding's mechanism note states this
explicitly ("Six logistic regressions with 9 features each = 54 effective
parameters... the classifiers overfit to macro state and ignore the much-
noisier arc-Sharpe persistence signal that inverse-vol picks up
structurally without any fitting" —
`apps/docs/docs/findings/meta-allocator-regime-forecasting.md`).

A feature stack that would *not* be circular: macro-only, no arc-internal
features. The cost is throwing away the only feature with non-trivial
predictive power (trailing Sharpe). Expected outcome: classifier collapses
to near-constant prediction; soft vote collapses to ≈ 1/N. This is the
right experiment to run *if* the question is "does macro state predict
arc-Sharpe-state" in isolation — but the answer from the
`findings/macro-regime-diagnostic.md` page is already partial-OOS, and from
`findings/per-regime-universe-oracle.md` (entropy 2.63 / 2.81 bits — 94%
of max) is closer to confirmed-null at the arc-selection granularity.

## 4. Classifier family

The C3 pre-reg used logistic regression (no sklearn dep — implemented
directly). This is the right choice for the workspace:

- **Random forest (AFML default)** — requires sklearn, ≥ 1000 trees for
  stable OOS calibration on binary financial labels (Krauss et al. 2017
  *EJOR* 259:689 on S&P 500 stock classification), and the per-arc
  training set of ≈ 200 labels is well below the regime where RFs
  outperform logistic. Skip.
- **Tinygrad MLP** — available via `apps/factor`'s runtime. At our
  sample size the 9-feature → 1-output MLP would underperform logistic
  by 5-10% (folklore from Caruana et al. 2008 *ICML*: trees-beat-MLPs
  on tabular until ~10k+ obs). Skip.
- **Statsmodels logit** — available in `pairs`'s [research] extra. Gives
  proper p-values and confidence intervals on coefficients; useful for
  the *diagnostic* of "which features are doing the work" but not
  required for the allocator itself.
- **Numpy logistic with L2 reg, λ chosen by walk-forward CV** — this is
  what C3 implemented. Lightest weight. Correct choice.

**No new package is needed.** The implementation cost to rebuild C3 with
point-in-time thresholds and uniqueness weighting is ≈ 1 day of work in
`apps/docs/scripts/meta_allocator_run.py`. The implementation cost is not
the bottleneck — the question is whether the expected delta justifies
re-running it.

## 5. CSCV / DSR deflation requirement

The C3 run charged `n_trials = 8` (5 modeling candidates + 3 hyperparameter
lookbacks on persistence). For a re-run with:

- Threshold variants (point-in-time vs full-sample) × 2
- H variants (21 / 63 / 126) × 3
- Feature-stack variants (macro-only / arc-only / combined) × 3
- Classifier-regularization sweep × 3

Effective N is ≈ 2 × 3 × 3 × 3 = **54 trials**, on top of the 8 already
charged. Per Bailey-López de Prado 2014 (SSRN 2460551), the DSR haircut on
the *best* of 54 effectively-independent trials at n_obs = 2740 daily obs
and σ_SR_estimate ≈ 0.25 (workspace baseline) is:

E[max₅₄ Z] / SR_estimator_sd ≈ √(2 ln 54) − γ/√(2 ln 54) ≈ 2.83 − 0.20
= **2.63 standard errors of inflation**.

A candidate's raw OOS ann-Sharpe needs to exceed B3's +1.722 by at least
2.63 × 0.25 = **+0.66 annualized** *before* DSR-correction to land
`confirmed-OOS` against the inverse-vol baseline. That puts the bar at
**Sharpe ≈ +2.38** annualized. For reference:

- The arc with the highest annual Sharpe in the repo is vol_v3-dolthub
  at +1.68 over n=29 obs on a 33-month window with no vol crisis
  (`findings/vol-v3-dolthub-oos.md`).
- DCA's full-sample is +1.93 over 5232 daily obs.
- The C3 raw run was +1.068.

For meta-labeling to land confirmed-OOS, it would need to deliver a
**+1.31 Sharpe lift over its 2026-05-23 raw number** — roughly the
distance between "noise" and "best-arc-in-repo on its best window." That's
not a plausible bar to clear with the same feature stack + a thresholding
fix + uniqueness weighting. The published Singh-Joubert lifts (~5-10%
classification precision) translate to maybe ~+0.2 to +0.4 Sharpe in their
single-instrument setting; the analogous lift here would land us at maybe
+1.3 to +1.5 raw Sharpe — still below B3's +1.722, still `reversed-OOS`.

**This is the most important number in the brief.** Even under a generous
literature-derived expected lift, the protocol cannot clear the deflated
bar set by the existing inverse-vol baseline.

## 6. Falsifiable pre-registration draft

If the user insists on running it anyway, this is the minimal pre-reg:

```
Hypothesis: A per-arc meta-labeling allocator with point-in-time
  Sharpe thresholds + AFML §4.5 sample-uniqueness weighting + macro-
  only features beats the inverse-arc-vol baseline B3 at 95% CI on
  pooled OOS 2015–2025.
Mechanism: The 2026-05-23 C3 result (Sharpe +1.068 vs B3 +1.722) was
  contaminated by (i) using full-sample Sharpe as label threshold —
  test-set leakage; (ii) ignoring observation-uniqueness; (iii)
  arc-internal trailing-Sharpe features re-introducing persistence
  signal that inverse-vol already captures structurally. Removing
  all three eliminates the dominant noise paths.
Universe: meta-alloc-arcs-6 (dca, gate, pairs, relational,
  dca_winner_4etf, vol_v3) — same as 2026-05-23.
Windowing: meta-alloc-3fold-2015-25 — same as 2026-05-23.
Feature stack: ss_macro 6-stack ONLY (fed_funds, slope_10y_3m,
  credit_baa, m2_yoy, real_yield_10y, vix). No arc-internal features.
Label: y_{i,t} = 1 if forward-63d Sharpe of arc i exceeds the
  trailing-252d-expanding-window Sharpe of arc i at train-end
  (point-in-time, not full-sample).
Classifier: numpy L2-logistic, λ ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1.0}, walk-forward CV
  on training set only. Sample weights = avg_uniqueness per AFML §4.5.
Decision metric: pooled OOS ann Sharpe; DSR-t (n_trials=12 — 3 λ × 4
  ablations); Ledoit-Wolf 95% CI on ΔSR vs B3.
Expected delta vs B3: −0.20 to +0.10 (literature-derived from Singh-
  Joubert ≈ 5-10% classifier-precision lift → ≈ +0.2 raw Sharpe;
  applied to C3's −0.65 starting deficit leaves us still below B3).
What counts as confirmed-OOS: CI excludes 0 on positive side AND
  DSR-t > +3.0 AND ΔSR ≥ +0.3 vs B3.
What counts as confirmed-null: CI includes 0 AND |ΔSR| < +0.05.
Anything else: partial / reversed.
```

The expected-delta line is the most important and the one that makes
this pre-reg an honest one: **the literature does not justify a magnitude
that clears the +0.3 bar**. Writing the pre-reg this way reveals that
the experiment is *expected* to land partial-null on prior-art grounds —
which by the repo's own pre-registration discipline
(`CLAUDE.md` §"Writing pre-registrations") is the wrong reason to run it.

## 7. Failure modes and false-positive risks

From the published replications and our own meta-allocator finding:

- **Singh-Joubert (2022) caveat — base signal quality matters.** They
  explicitly note "if the algorithm is bad then meta-labeling would
  likely only reduce the downside." Our 6 arcs are weak base signals
  (only ~9/146 leaderboard rows are confirmed-OOS — see
  `apps/docs/docs/findings/deflated-sharpe-leaderboard.md`). Meta-
  labeling cannot manufacture lift the underlying arcs do not have.
- **Joubert-Sepp-Petukhina 2024** (Hudson-Thames working paper, *Three
  Algorithms for Combining Meta-Labels*) finds that **simple-mean
  combination of meta-labels works better than stacked classifiers
  when arcs are weakly correlated** — but this is *within* a single
  primary signal across multiple meta-labelers, not across multiple
  primaries. Our adaptation has the wrong direction of the matrix.
- **The strongest false-positive risk**: pick a feature stack that
  inadvertently encodes the test-set Sharpe (e.g. trailing-Sharpe
  features) and report a lift that vanishes under stricter purging.
  Already happened in our C3.
- **The strongest false-negative risk**: use a label horizon that
  doesn't match the arc's natural decision horizon. vol_v3 makes
  decisions monthly; dca makes them quarterly; relational daily. A
  single H = 63d label is wrong for ≥ 2/3 of the arcs.

The López de Prado literature is consistent on this: **meta-labeling
amplifies, it does not create**. The repo's leaderboard says we don't
have enough underlying signal for amplification to clear the deflated bar.

## 8. EV ranking against alternatives

Three candidates for the same compute budget:

(a) **Fresh inverse-arc-vol walk-forward, vol_v3 excluded.** The
2026-05-23 meta-allocator finding's own "highest-EV follow-up" — drop
vol_v3 from the panel and re-test whether B3's +0.367 lift vs B2 still
clears 95% CI. If yes, the inverse-vol result is panel-structural and
deployment-ready (this is what shifts the Tier-1 recommendation from
"DCA-alone" to "inverse-vol across 5 arcs"). If no, the result is a
vol_v3-2024-25-window artifact and DCA-alone stays canonical.
**Engineering cost: ~2 hours** (one driver edit + re-run on existing
scripts). **Probability the result changes what we deploy: high
(~40%).** Highest information-density experiment available.

(b) **Kritzman-Page-Turkington K=2 turbulence overlay as risk-off
scalar.** Not as an arc selector (the 2026-05-23 C2 run already
falsified that — turbulence overlay reduces exposure right when vol_v3
is firing, inverting the intended sign). Instead, apply turbulence to
the **dca sleeve only** as a 50% exposure haircut in turbulent regimes.
This matches the Kritzman 2012 paper's actual claim (FAJ 2012 — risk-
off in turbulence on a stock+bond panel) and isolates the turbulence
gate from the arc-selection contamination. **Engineering cost: ~1
day.** **Probability the result changes what we deploy: medium-low
(~15%) — the published lift is real but small (Kritzman reports ~30%
DD reduction at ~unchanged Sharpe) and our base canonical DCA already
has a max DD of −40.7% (acceptable per `apps/dca/`'s pre-reg).**

(c) **Meta-labeling per arc (this brief's protocol).** Even with the
threshold fix + uniqueness weighting + macro-only features, the
literature-derived expected lift (≈ +0.2 raw Sharpe) on top of the
C3 starting point (−0.65 deficit vs B3) lands at ≈ −0.45 vs B3 — still
`reversed-OOS`. **Engineering cost: ~1 day.** **Probability the
result changes what we deploy: very low (~3%) — the result is
literature-predicted negative and the C3 evidence supports the prior.**

**Ranking by EV-per-unit-engineering-cost:**

1. **(a) Inverse-vol robustness check** — runaway winner. 2 hours, ~40%
   chance to change deployment.
2. **(b) Turbulence overlay on DCA-only** — ~1 day, ~15% chance.
3. **(c) Meta-labeling re-run** — ~1 day, ~3% chance. Dominated.

## Recommendation

**Deprioritize meta-labeling per arc. Run (a) first; if (a) lifts B3 to
panel-structural-confirmed, deploy inverse-vol-5-arc. If (a) collapses,
DCA-alone stays Tier-1.** (b) is a reasonable second experiment if the
user wants a *risk-targeting* knob rather than a return-prediction one —
and the published evidence base for (b) is materially stronger than for
(c).

The user's instinct to invoke López de Prado is right at a high level
("there should be a per-arc decision layer") but wrong at the protocol
level — canonical meta-labeling is a primary/secondary *sizing* pipeline
on a single instrument, not a cross-strategy *selection* pipeline, and
our 2026-05-23 attempt to adapt it landed exactly where the literature
brief and the sample-size analysis predicted it would.

The honest framing for the productionization conversation: **the
allocation problem across our 6 arcs has been worked over four
independent angles** (additive ensembles, per-window meta-allocator, per-
regime universe oracle, meta-allocator regime forecasting). The
convergent finding is that **inverse-arc-vol is the only allocator that
beats 1/N at 95% CI on the pooled OOS data**, and that result *may* be
vol_v3-window-specific (we don't know yet). Until (a) is run, the right
deployment is DCA-alone. After (a), it's either inverse-vol-5-arc
(if it holds) or DCA-alone (if it doesn't). Meta-labeling does not
plausibly enter the deployment conversation at this evidence level.
