# StockSurvey Methodology — Assumptions Audit

**Auditor:** Claude (read-only). **Date:** 2026-05-23. **Branch:** `dsr-cross-arc-leaderboard`.
**Scope:** Systematic enumeration of load-bearing assumptions baked into the
DSR cross-arc ladder, the per-arc dumpers feeding it, and the
findings/leaderboard adjudication layer at
`apps/docs/docs/`. Read-only — no code or docs modified.

Companion docs already on disk:
- `.code-review-findings.md` — silent-correctness bugs (CB-1…CB-9, LA-1…LA-4, UF-1…UF-6).
- `.audit-thresholds.md` — 27 numeric thresholds, 5 distinct deployment cuts
  collapsed into "the paper-trade threshold".

This audit is intentionally orthogonal: not bugs, not thresholds, but the
**assumed scalars and conventions** that aren't even visible as numbers in
the prose. Those are the ones that silently corrupt the DSR ladder.

## 1. TL;DR — top 5 load-bearing assumptions ranked by leaderboard-invalidation risk

1. **`sharpe_std_ann = 0.25` is the single deflation calibration for every arc
   on the ladder** (`apps/docs/scripts/compute_dsr.py:57`). Sourced from 39
   factor walk-forward arms — i.e. **one arc class** — yet applied to gate,
   pairs, DCA, vol, relational, lie-shape-kNN, momentum, low-vol-BAB, factor
   long/short. Factor sweeps almost certainly have **wider** trial dispersion
   than gate sweeps (more degrees of freedom on head/loss/horizon) and **narrower**
   than vol/lie (heterogeneous structural variations). Severity 5: the
   deflated-t magnitude that drives the ranking is linear in `sharpe_std_ann`.
   If true dispersion is 0.40, every n_trials>1 row sees its deflation grow
   by 60% and the "DCA closest to t=+2" / "vol-v3-dolthub close to +3" claims
   need reproving.

2. **`periods_per_year` is set inconsistently across block-return arcs.**
   Verified by inspection of `Output/*-returns.npz`:
   - `lie-shape-knn-returns.npz`: **`ppy=12.0`** (n=57 blocks)
   - `lie-shape-knn-wide-returns.npz`: **`ppy=12.0`** (n=94 blocks)
   - `momentum-12-1-returns.npz`: **`ppy=12.0`** (n=302 blocks)
   - `low-vol-bab-returns.npz`: **`ppy=12.0`** (n=302 blocks)
   - `vol-returns.npz`: **`ppy=12.6`**
   - `vol-v3-dolthub-oos-returns.npz`: **`ppy=12.6`**
   - All daily streams (DCA, gate, pairs, relational): `ppy=252.0`
   `12.0` vs `12.6` is a `sqrt(12.6/12.0)=1.025` mis-annualization on the
   block-return arcs that use 12.0 — small (2.5% Sharpe shift) but **wrong in
   one direction across half the block arcs**. Severity 4: shifts every
   block-return Sharpe and DSR by a known constant for those rows; doesn't
   flip rankings but biases the ladder. The convention `252/20=12.6` is what
   `compute_dsr.py:269-270` expects implicitly via the `sharpe_std_pp =
   sharpe_std_ann/sqrt(ppy)` calc.

3. **`n_trials` is reconstructed by hand from arc history, with the explicit
   policy "round up when unsure" but no actual audit of the history.**
   (`compute_dsr.py:62-64`.) Anti-conservative direction is well-documented
   (under-counting → under-penalizes → inflates DSR), but the comment
   actually says "we round up when unsure" — which protects the **conservative**
   direction. The audit risk runs the other way: arcs with rich history but
   thin reconstruction (e.g. `pairs-v0 n_trials=4`, `gate-v0 n_trials=6`, `dca
   n_trials=4`) have likely **under-counted** because they cite only the
   single most-recent sweep. Severity 4: directly multiplicative on
   `expected_max_sharpe` via the Z-quantile.

4. **`borrow_bps_yr = 50` with implicit `gross_short = 0.5`** applies to
   `factor-5d-LS-skip1-{conservative,focused}` and `lie-shape-knn-LS{,-wide}`.
   The `0.5` is hardcoded at `compute_dsr.py:263` and assumes the L/S book
   is built so `sum(|w|) ≈ 1` and gross short is half. `momentum-12-1` and
   `low-vol-bab` declare borrow already in-stream (`borrow_bps_yr=0`), so they
   bypass this — but if their in-stream charge uses a different convention,
   the cross-arc comparison is silently mis-calibrated. **No code-level
   assertion that `sum(|w|)≈1` for the streams using the aggregator-level
   borrow.** Severity 3: small absolute Sharpe shift; matters at the t=+2
   threshold.

5. **Walk-forward splits and n_obs are heterogeneous across the rows being
   ranked.** Confirmed by NPZ inspection: n_obs ranges from **30**
   (`vol-returns.npz`) to **5245** (`dca-winner-4etf-returns.npz`). DSR's
   length-aware term `sqrt(n_obs - 1)` partially handles this, but the
   *higher-moment* terms (skew, kurtosis) are estimated on n=30 for vol vs
   n=5245 for DCA — the kurtosis estimate at n=30 has SE ~`sqrt(24/30)≈0.9`,
   meaning the DSR denominator for vol-v3 carries a ±100% kurtosis estimation
   noise that becomes part of the published t-stat. Severity 4: explains why
   vol-v3 sees a fat-tail penalty that may be a sampling artifact, not a real
   higher-moment.

**Six through ten** (one-line summaries — full treatment below):
6. `Phase-2 = 21 mega-caps` defined in `apps/relational/src/relational/sectors.py:98` is
   load-bearing for the **entire** relational arc plus lie-shape-kNN narrow; the
   universe-shift finding already showed this is fragile but the ladder treats both
   narrow and wide rows as comparable.
7. `factor-narrow = 297 names` construction is undocumented in `compute_dsr.py`
   — survivorship-free or curated?
8. Stooq archive's coverage of pre-2005 international ETFs and delisted equities
   is assumed but not verified for the DCA basket arc (already flagged today).
9. `commission_bps=10` is universal across arc dumpers, while options spreads
   (vol) and microcap spreads (lie-shape narrow) are realistically 100-500 bps
   and 50-100 bps respectively. The ladder compares apples (ETF DCA at
   realistic 10 bps) to pears (vol at unrealistic 10 bps).
10. `deflated_tstat > 2` as significance bar is psychology convention, not
    multiple-testing-aware. Bonferroni on ~17 rows → Z>+2.7; FDR-controlled at
    5% gives Z>+2.4-ish. The published bar is anti-conservative.

## 2. Per-assumption catalog

### Category A — Calibration assumptions (DSR-mechanical, highest leverage)

#### A1. `sharpe_std_ann = 0.25` is the global deflation dispersion
- **File:** `apps/docs/scripts/compute_dsr.py:57`; converted at line 270:
  `sharpe_std_pp = spec.sharpe_std_ann / math.sqrt(ppy)`.
- **What's assumed:** the cross-trial dispersion of *annualized* per-period
  Sharpe estimates within each arc's search is ≈0.25, regardless of arc class.
- **Sourcing:** "empirically-observed cross-trial std (0.245) across 39
  factor-arc walk-forward arms". That's **one arc class** generalized to all.
- **Invalidator:** measure cross-trial Sharpe dispersion within at least one
  non-factor arc (gate v0 threshold sweep, vol v3 grid, DCA basket Optuna
  trials). If any class's dispersion differs by >2× from 0.25, the constant
  is wrong per-arc.
- **Sensitivity:** linear in `expected_max_sharpe`, which subtracts from
  `sharpe_per_period`. For DCA at n_trials=4, E[maxSR] ≈ 0.25 × 1.05 ≈ 0.26
  annualized; doubling `sharpe_std_ann` to 0.50 ≈ doubles E[maxSR] to ≈0.52,
  cutting DCA's deflated-t by roughly the ratio of (DCA ann Sharpe − E[maxSR])
  to its current value. DCA's published deflated-t of ~+1.93 plausibly
  drops below +1 under `sharpe_std_ann=0.45`.
- **Severity:** 5.
- **Test design:**
  1. For each arc with a logged Optuna study or sweep grid, dump all trial
     val-Sharpes.
  2. Compute `std(ddof=1)` of the *annualized* trial Sharpes per arc.
  3. Compare to 0.25 anchor; substitute per-arc `sharpe_std_ann` in the
     SPECS list.
  4. Re-run `compute_dsr.py` and diff the ladder.
- **Compute cost:** ~1 day. The Optuna studies for DCA basket / vol-v3 are
  already on disk; factor sweeps need NPZ aggregation.

#### A2. `n_trials` reconstructed by hand; `note` says "round up when unsure"
- **File:** `apps/docs/scripts/compute_dsr.py:62-64`.
- **What's assumed per row:**
  | arc | n_trials | likely under/over |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | gate-v0-drawdown | 6 | **under** — q sweep + gate-mode + horizon variants documented elsewhere |
  | pairs-v0 | 4 | **under** — `pairs-classical-v0.md` shows EG-p threshold + z-thresh + spread-window grid; 4 is the canonical config count, not the trial count |
  | factor-indicator-baseline-LO | 50 | **probably right** but blends factor-arc's entire search history into "the indicator baseline's trial count" |
  | relational-analog-cross-ticker | 16 | **under** — Phase-2 8-arm scoreboard × DWT/raw × cross/per-ticker, but the relational arc has more pivots than that (Morlet, DWT-fail, etc.) |
  | vol-v3-regime-gated | 12 | **probably right** for v0→v3.1 progression |
  | dca-canonical | 4 | **dramatically under** — Phase 4a/b/c/d is 4 *named* baskets, but the universe choice (13 ETFs vs other candidates) is itself a search; honestly closer to 20-30 |
  | dca-basket-search-winner-4etf | 200 | **right** — explicit Optuna budget |
  | lie-shape-knn-LS | 9 | **probably right** for v1/v3/v4 |
  | factor-5d-LS-skip1-conservative | 50 | conservative framing |
  | factor-5d-LS-skip1-focused | 8 | focused — coexists in same SPECS list (two framings of same row) |
  | momentum-12-1 / low-vol-bab | 1 | correct — pre-registered |
- **Invalidator:** for each arc, walk the leaderboard rows associated with
  the arc and count every distinct config that was empirically tested.
- **Sensitivity:** `E[maxSR] ∝ Z(1 - 1/T)`, so doubling T from 4 to 8 shifts
  the Gumbel quantile from Z(0.75)=0.674 to Z(0.875)=1.15 — a 70% increase
  in E[maxSR]. DCA at n_trials=8 instead of 4 cuts its DSR markedly.
- **Severity:** 4.
- **Test design:** for each arc with prose history, build the trial inventory
  (file + commit-history grep for distinct configs); produce a single audit
  spreadsheet; re-run.
- **Compute cost:** ~half day of reading.

#### A3. `expected_max_sharpe` uses the Bailey-Lopez de Prado Gumbel approximation
- **File:** `packages/portfolio/src/ss_portfolio/deflated.py:80-98`.
- **What's assumed:** trial Sharpes are *iid normal*. Their max converges to a
  Gumbel with the closed-form `(1-γ)Z(1-1/T) + γZ(1-1/(Te))`.
- **Invalidator:** correlated trials (same data, different hyperparams) violate
  IID. Bailey & López de Prado themselves flag this; the practical fix is the
  "effective number of independent trials" — typically much smaller than the
  raw count. **Under-correction is anti-conservative** (we're using raw T
  which over-counts).
- **Sensitivity:** moderate — going from T=50 to T_effective=10 shifts E[maxSR]
  by 30-40%.
- **Severity:** 3.
- **Test design:** compute Spearman-rho between trial val-Sharpes across the
  factor sweep; estimate effective T via the Cheverud / Li-Ji eigenvalue
  approach.

#### A4. Per-period dispersion derivation: `sharpe_std_pp = sharpe_std_ann / sqrt(ppy)`
- **File:** `apps/docs/scripts/compute_dsr.py:270`.
- **What's assumed:** annualized Sharpe estimates have variance that scales as
  `1/ppy`. This is correct **if** trial-to-trial Sharpe shifts are uncorrelated
  in time — same IID assumption as A3. For block-Sharpe arcs (e.g. vol at
  n=30, ppy=12.6), `sharpe_std_pp = 0.25 / sqrt(12.6) ≈ 0.0705`.
- **Invalidator:** trial Sharpes are computed on the **same** OOS sample;
  there's no time dimension to average over. The conversion is
  conceptually wrong — `sharpe_std_ann` is already "annualized cross-trial
  dispersion of OOS-block-Sharpe estimates"; dividing by sqrt(ppy) inverts
  the same scaling that produced the annualized version. Worth a head-on
  derivation check.
- **Severity:** 4 if the derivation is genuinely wrong; the entire deflation
  magnitude collapses by a factor of `sqrt(ppy)`.
- **Test design:** synthetic-data check — generate trials with known per-period
  Sharpe distribution, compute annualized Sharpes, compute true cross-trial
  std at both per-period and annualized scales, verify the sqrt(ppy)
  relationship matches.

#### A5. `periods_per_year` chosen per-arc by convention; inconsistent across block arcs
- **File:** per-arc dumpers; observable in NPZ.
- **What's set:** `lie/momentum/low-vol-bab = 12.0`, `vol = 12.6`. The
  canonical 20-day-block annualization is `252/20 = 12.6`; using 12.0 implies
  21-day blocks or just a rounded value.
- **Invalidator:** read the rebal cadence from each arc's run script and
  cross-check.
- **Sensitivity:** `ann_sharpe ∝ sqrt(ppy)`, so a 5% ppy error shifts ann
  Sharpe by 2.5%. Across 4 affected rows, this is a small but constant
  bias.
- **Severity:** 3.
- **Test design:** trivial — read source, audit NPZ, fix dumpers, regenerate.

### Category B — Cost assumptions

#### B1. `commission_bps = 10` is universal across arcs that have very different microstructure
- **Files:** ~15+ sites in `apps/relational/`, `apps/factor/`, `apps/pairs/`,
  `apps/dca/scripts/build_checkpoint.py:51` (5 bps), `apps/cfr/`.
- **What's assumed:** every arc's friction is 10 bps round-trip on dollar
  turnover. Reality:
  - DCA (13 ETF basket): 10 bps is **conservative-high**; ETF spreads are typically
    0.5-3 bps on liquid SPDRs. The 4-ETF basket Optuna search would *gain* if
    repriced at 2 bps.
  - factor-narrow long-only over 297 names: 10 bps mixes mega-caps (1-3 bps) with
    smaller names (15-30 bps); the weighted-mean cost is plausibly 8-12 bps.
  - lie-shape-kNN Phase-2 (21 mega-caps): 10 bps is **high**; mega-caps trade
    at 1-3 bps. **The Phase-2 arc is being penalized.**
  - vol-v3 (options): 10 bps is **wildly low**. Realistic options bid-ask is
    100-500 bps per strangle, plus dealer markups. Vol-v3's published Sharpe
    is plausibly +1 to +2 lower under realistic options friction.
  - pairs (5-bps, 2 leg-flips per trade): 10 bps per leg = 20 bps per round
    trip — high for the mega-cap pairs universe but plausible if it includes
    less-liquid cointegrating pairs.
- **Sensitivity:**
  - DCA: small (5 bps shift on 4-12 rebals/yr × 50% turnover = 1-3 bps/yr).
  - vol-v3: catastrophic. Vol-v3's `full_panel_alpha` of +0.557% per block ×
    12.6 blocks/yr × 100% turnover ≈ ~7% annual gross. At 200 bps round-trip
    × 2 trades/yr = 4% friction. The fired-only book at +1.52% × 6 blocks ≈
    9% gross could lose half to realistic friction.
- **Severity:** 5 specifically for vol-v3; 3 elsewhere.
- **Test design:**
  1. Per-arc cost-grid sensitivity: re-run with `commission_bps ∈ {2, 5, 10,
     20, 50}` for each long-only/L-S arc.
  2. For vol-v3 specifically: re-run with `commission_bps ∈ {10, 100, 200,
     400}` representing real strangle friction.
  3. Plot DSR-ladder ranking as a function of commission grid; verdicts that
     persist across the grid are robust.

#### B2. `borrow_bps_yr = 50` with hardcoded `gross_short = 0.5`
- **File:** `apps/docs/scripts/compute_dsr.py:263` —
  `strat = strat - (borrow_bps_yr/1e4) * (1.0/ppy) * 0.5`.
- **What's assumed:** L/S book is L1-normalized to ~1, so gross-short is
  exactly half.
- **Invalidator:** the `block_port_returns_long_short_np` constructor at the
  factor side does L1-normalize, but `lie-shape-knn` and any future
  constructor doesn't necessarily.
- **Severity:** 3.
- **Test design:** for each L/S arc, dump the per-block `sum(|w|)` and
  `sum(max(-w, 0))` arrays; verify gross-short is actually 0.5±ε.

#### B3. Cost charging convention varies per arc
- **Files:** `apps/factor/src/factor/objectives.py:470` (`0.5 * L1(Δw)` for LO,
  `L1(Δw)` for LS); `apps/pairs/scripts/run_walkforward.py` (`bps × 2`);
  `apps/dca` (`PassiveEW` cost convention).
- **What's assumed:** these all measure "cost on per-period net return" the
  same way. They don't — the L1 conventions differ (turnover vs L1 of weight
  change), the per-leg semantics differ.
- **Severity:** 3. Already flagged in CB cross-cutting #2 + #7.
- **Test design:** synthetic round-trip — apply each cost function to the
  same `(w_t, w_t+1)` series and compute realized cost. Differences > 1 bp are
  worth fixing.

#### B4. Spread / impact / slippage = 0 for everything
- **Files:** universal absence.
- **What's assumed:** no spread cost, no market impact, no slippage. Only
  realized cost is `commission_bps × turnover`.
- **Invalidator:** vol-v3's `live_infrastructure_unbuilt` admission in
  CLAUDE.md is implicit acknowledgement that the ladder is comparing
  paper-friction backtests to a deployable arc (DCA) whose own paper-friction
  is also unrealistically low.
- **Severity:** 4 for vol-v3 and pairs; 2 for ETF-only arcs.

### Category D — Walk-forward / sample-window assumptions

#### D1. Heterogeneous train/val splits across arcs
- **Per arc:**
  - DCA Optuna: train **2005-02-25 → 2018-12-31** / val **2019-01-01 → 2025**
    (single split, no rolling).
  - vol-v3-DoltHub: 300d train / 120d val rolling, step 120 (non-overlap).
  - vol-v3 original: same rolling structure on gauss314.
  - factor walk-forward: 63 train blocks / 39 val blocks / step 39 — i.e.
    ~5y train / 3y val rolling.
  - gate v0: 1260d train / 780d val / step 780.
  - pairs v0: same 6-window structure as factor / gate.
  - relational Phase-2: single train/val split with `phase-2 split` tag.
- **What's assumed:** the DSR cross-arc ladder compares rows whose `n_obs`
  reflects fundamentally different sampling regimes:
  - rolling (factor, gate, pairs) — multiple non-overlapping val windows
    each contributing their own block-stream
  - single-split (DCA, relational Phase-2) — one continuous val sample
  - vol-v3 — rolling but block-aggregated to per-rebal stream
- **Invalidator:** the higher-moment estimates (skew, kurtosis) used in the
  DSR denominator are estimated on the entire `n_obs` stream as if it were
  one sample. Rolling-window streams have **window-aggregation bias** (each
  val window's mean is roughly independent; their pooled second moment
  reflects within-window + between-window variance) that single-split
  streams don't.
- **Severity:** 4.
- **Test design:** for each rolling arc, also dump the per-window mean and
  std, and compute `between_window_var / pooled_var` to characterize the
  bias.

#### D2. Forward-target overlap with rebal cadence (the vol-v3 cadence bug, generalized)
- **The bug pattern:** if forward returns are sampled every `rebal_days`
  bars but the forward window is `> rebal_days`, the sample has overlapping
  forward windows that are **not iid** even though the indexing looks
  independent.
- **Already caught in:** vol-v3-DoltHub original.
- **Risk in other arcs:**
  - `apps/factor/factor/train.py:241-243` — `forward_skip > 0` creates
    label overlap; flagged as LA-1.
  - `apps/gate/` — drawdown forecast at horizon 20d sampled every 20d? Check.
  - `apps/pairs/` — pair returns aggregated daily, but trade triggers fire
    on z-cross with no explicit non-overlap rail.
  - `apps/factor/scripts/sh-indicator-r5-s1-windows.npz` — 5-day horizon
    sampled at 5-day cadence; non-overlap by construction (good).
  - `apps/dca` — 5232 daily bars, no forward window: not applicable.
  - `lie-shape-knn` — 21-day horizon ($H=21$), 20-day rebal-cadence? Check.
- **Severity:** 5 for any arc where it fires; 3 as a forward-looking risk.
- **Test design:** for each block-return NPZ, document the
  `(rebal_days, forward_window_days)` pair; flag any where forward > rebal.

#### D3. `min_history` filter creates survivorship in regime trainer
- **Already flagged** as LA-4. Severity depends on `load_stooq_matrix`'s
  delisted-ticker handling.

#### D4. Walk-forward "silent skip" when fewer windows fit than expected
- **Already flagged** as UF-5. Severity 2.

### Category E — Statistical / methodology assumptions

#### E1. DSR formula assumes IID per-period returns
- **What's assumed:** the per-period return stream `r_t` is iid (with
  finite skew/kurtosis adjusted).
- **Reality:** every strategy in the workspace has clustered vol and
  serial dependence in returns. The Sharpe-estimator distribution under
  serial dependence shrinks the *effective* n_obs by a factor of
  `(1-ρ)/(1+ρ)` where `ρ` is lag-1 autocorrelation. For block-rebal arcs
  the rebal cadence helps, but for daily arcs (DCA, gate, pairs daily-agg,
  relational) it's a real concern.
- **Severity:** 3.

#### E2. `probabilistic_sharpe` NaN-on-degenerate-denom
- **File:** `deflated.py:117-119`.
- **Already in code review as CB-issue.** Severity 2.

#### E3. Normality of the trial-max distribution (Gumbel approximation)
- **Already covered in A3.** Severity 3.

#### E4. Block-Sharpe vs daily-Sharpe comparability in `compute_dsr`
- **Files:** `ss_portfolio.sharpe.block_sharpe_with_costs` vs
  `factor.objectives.block_sharpe` vs the DSR's `standardize_oos`
  computing Sharpe from a flat per-period array.
- **What's assumed:** all three compute the same Sharpe given the same
  per-period stream.
- **Risk:** cost conventions differ (B3); the DSR aggregator's Sharpe
  computation discards the cost layer assumption and just operates on the
  net stream — so the cost convention bias propagates from the dumper,
  not the aggregator.
- **Severity:** 2 (uniform bias if uniform convention; cross-arc bias
  otherwise).

  per-row claims.

## 5. Top 5-10 unaudited assumptions worth testing next

Ranked by `severity × probability of being wrong × cost to test`.

### Priority 1 — Per-arc `sharpe_std_ann` measurement (A1)

- **Hypothesis:** the trial-Sharpe dispersion varies by ≥2× across arc
  classes; using 0.25 globally biases the ladder against arcs with high
  natural dispersion and toward arcs with low dispersion.
- **Pre-reg:** for each arc with logged Optuna study or sweep grid:
  (1) compute std of annualized val-Sharpe across all trials;
  (2) substitute the empirical value per-arc in SPECS;
  (3) re-run DSR ladder.
- **Expected outcome:** I'd bet DCA's dispersion is ~0.05-0.10 (small ETF
  basket changes don't move Sharpe much), vol-v3's is ~0.40-0.60
  (heterogeneous structural variants), factor's is the original 0.25.
  If true: DCA's DSR is **understated** (lower E[maxSR] is appropriate),
  vol-v3's is **overstated** (its big move from v0 to v3 is partly trial-noise).
- **Test cost:** half day reading Optuna logs + 30 min to re-run ladder.

  scales; check the sqrt-relationship.
- **Test cost:** 1 hour.

## 6. Honest caveats — is the ladder a sensible framework?

**The DSR-deflated-t ranking has three orthogonal problems even after every
finding above is addressed.**

### 6a. Heterogeneous arc types are not comparable on a single scalar

The leaderboard ranks **standalone** strategies (DCA, vol-v3) against
**overlays** (gate-v0). The standalone-mode rows produce
deflated-t on the strategy's own net return; overlay-mode on
the *excess over benchmark*. These are different statistical quantities
with different scales — a +0.10 alpha overlay over a low-vol benchmark
can outscore a +0.10 absolute-Sharpe standalone strategy even though the
former requires the benchmark to *also* be deployed. The "single
cross-arc-comparable number" framing is aspirational; it's actually two
numbers stuck in one column.

### 6b. The deflation is one-tailed; the verdict vocabulary is two-tailed

`deflated_tstat` deflates by `expected_max_sharpe` — but the arc-history
that informs `n_trials` includes both *winners reported* and *losers
discarded*. The Bailey-Lopez de Prado framework implicitly assumes the
researcher reports their best trial; for prediction-problem-pivot arcs
that **report negative results** (pairs `confirmed-null`, factor loss-pivot
falsified), the deflation is over-counting trials that were never going
to be cherry-picked. **The ladder under-deflates winners and over-deflates
honest negatives.**

### 6c. The framework conflates "skill" with "deployability"

DSR measures `P(skill | observed Sharpe is best of T coin flips)`. That's
not the same as `P(this will work next year)`. A regime-specific signal
(2024-26 calm-bull short-vol tailwind for vol-v3) can clear DSR but be
non-deployable because regimes change. The leaderboard's verdict-label
layer (`confirmed-OOS` / `partial-OOS`) addresses this informally; the
DSR-ranking layer ignores it.

### 6d. Ranking on a noisy scalar produces ranking instability

With 17 rows and the per-row scalar carrying SE of ~0.3-0.5 (rough
estimate from kurtosis-estimation noise on n=30-300), the *order* of
adjacent rows is mostly stable but rows within ~1 σ of each other are
not meaningfully distinguishable. The leaderboard presentation shows
rank position as if it were precise. **Adjacent rows should be plotted
with their CI, not as a sorted list.**

### Joint recommendation with the methodology (walk-forward) audit

If the parallel `walkforward-apples-to-apples` audit (see
`.research-walkforward-apples-to-apples.md`) lands and confirms that
heterogeneous train/val splits are not directly comparable, the right
move is **not** to fix the ladder by aligning splits (that loses
information) but to **stratify the ladder** by:
1. Arc structural class (standalone vs overlay vs prediction-only).
2. Sample-window structure (single-split vs rolling-walk-forward).
3. Universe class (mega-cap-only vs broad vs ETF-only).

Within each stratum, DSR ranking is meaningful. Across strata, it's a
single-axis projection of a higher-dimensional comparison.
